\section{Method details}
\label{app:method}
\subsection{Artifact contents and sampling}
\label{app:sampling}
Equation~(1) of the main paper is the conditional mean; the joint draw is separate. The profile reported in the main paper samples with the symbolic kernel stored in the artifact; the separate neural-joint sampler is unused. The stored dependence is one choice from the compile search: independence, a Chow--Liu tree, a triangular autoregression, a sparse Gaussian copula, a factor model, a truncated vine, or a two-component mixture, and the reported artifacts span several of these families.

Training screens the inputs by absolute rank correlation with the target and keeps at most twelve. The artifact stores a marginal and a transform for each feature, the selected dependence, the residual, a noise model, and a per-feature missing rate. A draw samples a latent vector from the stored dependence, maps each coordinate through the standard normal distribution function, inverts the marginal, and inverts the feature transform; the residual then supplies $\hat{y}$. When the training target is not binary, the stored noise is heteroscedastic: a linear fit of the log absolute residual on up to four screened features, with the scale clamped between the lower and upper deciles of those absolute residuals. The sampled target adds Gaussian noise of that scale and is clamped to the unit interval of the training transform. Each feature is then replaced, independently, by a missing mark with its stored probability. Before encoding, the compiler quantizes the marginals, the dependence, the residual, and the noise model. The charged byte count of a DOPE artifact is the encoded model file plus \texttt{projection.json}; a baseline is charged the files its sampler reads, as listed in \cref{app:bytes}.

\subsection{Training freeze, compile search, and the reported profile}
\label{app:compile}
Training maps the screened inputs through the width-16 $\tanh$ layer and runs AdamW on the regression mean-squared error, with gradient clipping at $5$. The hidden weights and biases are then frozen, and the intercept, the linear skip, and the hidden readout are refit on the frozen features. Coefficients whose absolute value is below $10^{-5}$ are dropped from the skip.

The compile search records the proxy $0.45u + 0.25d + 0.30j$ and keeps the candidate with the smallest normalized gate shortfall, then the smallest artifact, then the lowest candidate id. It reads the fit rows only. A candidate record carries no release certificate, and no global DOPE family has been selected.

The reported research profile, \texttt{features12\_\allowbreak{}steps2048}, was chosen among four bounded research profiles (twelve or twenty-four screened inputs, $512$ or $2{,}048$ steps) on a six-lineage discovery panel and checked on six disjoint confirmation lineages, by validation retention at fit seed $11$. Those twelve lineages belong to the hundred-lineage panel, so the reported profile carries a mild selection on validation rows. The untuned trainer default, used for any other profile, sets the hidden width between \ProductionWidthLo\ and \ProductionWidthHi\ and trains for \ProductionSteps\ steps; the main paper reports it as a separate arm. Other neural shapes (micro-TVAEs, TinyMAT, joint TVAE, a masked transformer, and a TabSyn candidate) are outside this measurement, and the direct-rank TabDDPM candidate is a research comparator with no claim on the $10{,}240$-byte tier.

\subsection{Dataset sketch and router vector}
\label{app:sketch}
An index can store a fixed sketch of a training table, which a separate dataset-action router consumes; neither enters the generator reported in the main paper. \texttt{embed-dataset} min-max normalizes each training column onto the unit interval, leaves a missing entry missing, and never reads the official test. Each column becomes twelve content summaries: a missing rate, moments, quantiles, a unique-value share, a tail ratio, and the absolute association with the target. Columns are sorted by those summaries, variance first. A ten-number header records the task, the log row count, the log column count, and the shape of the target. Each of the twelve summaries is then reduced across columns to a six-number distribution (minimum, lower quartile, median, upper quartile, maximum, and mean), which is the $12\times 6$ block. The sketch then keeps the first $64$ columns and stores each column's twelve summaries, padding a shorter table with zeros, and absolute correlations among the kept columns collapse to one further six-number distribution. The width is therefore $10 + 12\times 6 + 64\times 12 + 6 = 856$. The router vector appends $24$ candidate states, each a hidden state of width $128$ plus $10$ action predictions, to that sketch, for $856 + 24\times(128+10) = 4{,}168$ dimensions. A neighbour found with the sketch is still fit with the reported profile and still charged in bytes.

\section{Release gate}
\label{app:gate}
\subsection{MFS-v2, the historical formula}
MFS-v2 is the release score that historical receipts pin. With component $c_i\in[0,1]$, weight $w_i>0$, $\epsilon=10^{-6}$, and $W=\sum_i w_i$,
\[
\mathrm{MFS\text{-}v2} = 100\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{W}\sum_i w_i\ln(\epsilon+c_i)\right)
\]
when the hard-gate map is non-empty, every hard gate passed, and every positive-weight component is finite and inside $[0,1]$; otherwise the scalar is null. Its weights are utility transfer $0.30$, driver fidelity $0.20$, distribution fidelity $0.20$, structure fidelity $0.15$, coverage realism $0.10$, and compactness $0.05$. Privacy has soft weight zero, and a membership failure, an attribute failure, or a copied row is a gate that utility cannot offset. Compactness is $\mathrm{clamp}(1-\mathrm{bytes}/10240,0,1)$, and an artifact past $10{,}240$ bytes fails the byte gate.

\subsection{What the components measure}
Utility transfer is a one-sided lower bound on retention, clamped into $[0,1]$; it is distinct from the median retention in the main paper. Driver fidelity is an equal mixture of feature-importance rank agreement and top-feature-set overlap between the real and the synthetic table. Distribution fidelity is half the minimum marginal fidelity, plus a quarter of the sliced-Wasserstein fidelity and a quarter of the maximum-mean-discrepancy fidelity. Structure fidelity is the minimum dependence fidelity. The driver term stored beside the panel is the same equal mixture, applied to absolute correlations with the target; it differs from the permutation-importance auditor in the v2 definition, and this paper reports no component value from it.

\subsection{MFS-v3 and representation closeness}
MFS-v3 adds representation closeness to the geometric mean, as in equation~(2) of the main paper. v3 utility transfer is the same one-sided bound on the Kumo large retention: the model is conditioned on the synthetic rows and scored on real holdout rows. Mitra and TabICL must be measured on the same mapped tables for the gate. Their retentions stay out of that bound, and a CatBoost retention cannot fill this component. Real fit rows, real holdout rows, and synthetic rows share one map, fit on the real fit rows only: numeric columns go to the open unit interval by an empirical mid-rank quantile, categorical levels are pre-encoded to fixed codes in that interval, and column names become HMAC digests. The map withholds the minimum and the maximum, and a second fit on the synthetic rows is a protocol failure.

Representation closeness asks whether Kumo large places the mapped synthetic table near the mapped real table. The distance is half the Euclidean length between the two unit-length vectors, so it lies in $[0,1]$, and closeness is one minus that distance divided by the distance to a matched null, clamped to $[0,1]$. The synthetic table must also sit closer than an independent-marginal table of the same size under the same map. The matched null is chosen among three independent-marginal tables drawn on the shared map, seeded by the first eight bytes, little-endian, of the SHA digest of the dataset, the method, the configuration, the sample seed, and the candidate index $0$, $1$, or $2$; candidates are ordered by ordinary linear mean squared error on the mapped holdout, then by index, and the middle candidate is the matched null. Those losses are fixed before any encoder runs. Mitra and TabICL must show the same sign as Kumo large on the gap against the independent-marginal table; a sign flip, a zero match distance, or a missing vector makes the component null. Neither is averaged into representation closeness. The licensed encoder set is Kumo large (OpenMDW and Apache), Mitra (Apache), and TabICL (BSD); Foundation, TabPFN, TabDPT, the project's Hyperion, and target-aware blocks are refused. TabDPT is refused because its pretraining tables overlap this panel; whether the pretraining corpora of the three admitted encoders overlap PMLB is unknown, so their retentions are reported as transfer measurements rather than as independent evidence.

A recipient keeps the generator and a hash manifest: a salt identifier, the normalizer name, the encoder pin, one digest per column, a dtype code, and the byte length. The manifest carries no row, header, category string, extremum, or encoder weight. A public sidecar that echoes a source string or an extremum fails the cleartext gate, and an exact copy of a real row fails its own gate. The near-copy floor is the $0.01$ quantile, with linear interpolation, of squared Euclidean distances from each distinct real fit row to the nearest other distinct real fit row on the shared map; a synthetic row below that floor, an undefined floor, or a charge above $10{,}240$ bytes makes the scalar null.

\subsection{Executable conformance}
\label{app:conformance}
The release score uses the contract's weighted geometric mean with a component floor, clipped to $[0,100]$; the components lie in $[0,1]$ and a failed gate yields a null scalar. The matched null is the median linear-MSE candidate among three deterministic independent-marginal tables. This is a median selector; a learnability band is outside this implementation. The near-copy floor is the declared quantile of nearest-other squared distances among distinct real fit rows on the shared map, with linear interpolation, and undefined floors fail the gate. The scorer tests in \path{research/benchmark/tests/test_mfs_v3.py} verify exactly $100$ for a perfect profile, the byte boundary, and each prerequisite; the panel tests verify median null selection and the quantile floor. \cref{tab:conformance} lists the implemented fields, and \cref{tab:illustration} applies the bounded formula to invented components.

\section{Statistics}
\label{app:stats}
\subsection{Retention algebra and variance}
\label{app:retention-variance}
With $r$ the retention, the ratio rearranges to
\[
L_{\mathrm{synthetic}} = L_{\mathrm{real}} + (1-r)\bigl(L_{\mathrm{null}}-L_{\mathrm{real}}\bigr).
\]
\RetentionAlgebra
Let $D = L_{\mathrm{null}} - L_{\mathrm{real}}$, so $R = (L_{\mathrm{null}} - L_{\mathrm{synthetic}}) / D$ when $D \neq 0$. The partial derivatives are
\begin{align*}
\frac{\partial R}{\partial L_{\mathrm{null}}} &= \frac{L_{\mathrm{synthetic}} - L_{\mathrm{real}}}{D^{2}}, &
\frac{\partial R}{\partial L_{\mathrm{synthetic}}} &= -\frac{1}{D}, &
\frac{\partial R}{\partial L_{\mathrm{real}}} &= \frac{R}{D}.
\end{align*}
If the three losses have covariance $\Sigma$, the delta-method variance of one lineage's retention is $\nabla R^{\top} \Sigma \nabla R$, which diverges as $D$ approaches zero. The informativeness rule drops a lineage whose real improvement is below $0.01$ times the absolute null loss as a stability cut; the tables report bootstrap intervals rather than this standard error.

\subsection{Bootstrap, families, and equivalence}
\input{generated/tost-protocol.tex}
The v2 analyses follow \path{research/benchmark/review_fixes/predeclare_v2.json}, committed before any five-seed result existed. A fit value is the median of its three sample-seed retentions; a lineage value is the median of its complete fits (at least three for a five-seed arm); the panel level is the median of lineage values. Paired contrasts use the Hodges--Lehmann pseudo-median of lineage differences, which is the location the two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test addresses, and report the median difference beside it. Intervals come from a nested percentile bootstrap with $10{,}000$ draws: the four simulated name prefixes are clusters and every other lineage is its own cluster; a draw resamples clusters, then lineages inside each drawn cluster, then fit seeds inside each drawn lineage, separately for each arm. Holm families are declared per size: the primary family of $12$ tests, the density family of $9$, a deep-generator family of $12$, an other-generator family of $9$, and a control family of $6$; a member without data keeps its slot. Equivalence requires the $90\%$ nested interval of the Hodges--Lehmann difference to lie inside $\pm\VMargin$; a $20\%$ trimmed-mean (Yuen) test is a sensitivity. The v1 contract tested the mean paired difference with two one-sided $t$ tests at $\pm\TostEquivalenceMargin$ and unadjusted $\alpha=\TostAlpha$; paired retention differences have heavy tails when the denominator is small, so that estimand is kept only as a continuity column below.

A Friedman test and a Nemenyi critical difference compare the four density methods on their common complete-informative set, with rank $1$ for the higher retention; on CatBoost the Holm sign-test values are $\SignHolmGaussCb$, $\SignHolmChowCb$, and $\SignHolmIndCb$. A Bayesian signed-rank procedure would be an alternative to these frequentist tests and is not computed here.

\section{Extended related work}
\label{app:related}
\textbf{Generators by family.} Parametric copulas~\citep{patki2016sdv} and Chow--Liu trees~\citep{chow1968dependence} are the classical density models, and pair-copula constructions~\citep{aas2009paircopula} generalize them. Conditional GANs and variational autoencoders form a second family: CTGAN, TVAE, TableGAN, CTAB-GAN, and CTAB-GAN+~\citep{xu2019ctgan,park2018tablegan,zhao2021ctabgan,zhao2024ctabganplus}. Diffusion and score-based models form a third: TabDDPM, TabSyn, STASy, CoDi, TabDiff, CDTD, Forest-Diffusion, and GATD~\citep{kotelnikov2023tabddpm,zhang2024tabsyn,kim2023stasy,lee2023codi,shi2025tabdiff,mueller2025cdtd,jolicoeurmartineau2024forestdiffusion,zagardo2026gatd}. Autoregressive and language-model generators, and prior-data fitted generators, form a fourth~\citep{borisov2023great,solatorio2023realtabformer,ma2023tabpfgen,hollmann2025tabpfn}. Graphical neural generators are separate~\citep{liu2023goggle}; ARF estimates density with a forest~\citep{watson2023arf}; CART synthesis is the synthpop line~\citep{nowok2016synthpop}; SMOTE interpolates between neighbouring rows~\citep{chawla2002smote}; and a survey collects these design choices~\citep{borisov2024survey}. Probabilistic-program synthesis~\citep{saad2019bayesian} and CrossCat~\citep{mansinghka2016crosscat} produce small, inspectable joint models.

\textbf{How DOPE differs from each line.} A parametric copula or a Chow--Liu tree optimizes a joint density of a fixed form; DOPE adds an explicit model of the target's conditional mean and charges the result against a byte cap. A conditional GAN optimizes adversarial agreement and a VAE reconstruction plus a latent regularizer; both ship weights orders of magnitude larger than DOPE's cap. A diffusion model optimizes a denoising or score objective over the joint table; DOPE optimizes the target's conditional mean directly. A language-model generator optimizes serialized-row likelihood with a pretrained backbone; DOPE stores a screened residual of fixed width. A forest density model such as ARF optimizes axis-aligned splits of the joint table; CART synthesis optimizes sequential conditional draws, which also model the target given the features but store the trees. A prior-data fitted network optimizes a pretraining objective on other tables, whereas DOPE is fit on the lineage alone. A probabilistic-program synthesizer targets posterior inference over the joint distribution. A differentially private synthesizer optimizes under a privacy budget; DOPE's privacy evidence is empirical, and its release gate is the MFS contract.

\textbf{What this paper measured.} The measured generators are the Gaussian copula, a Chow--Liu baseline and independent marginals written for this comparison, CTGAN, TVAE, ARF at five fit seeds, TabSyn on the full panel with a scaled schedule, and a historical Forest-Flow block; TabDDPM, TabDiff, a language-model generator, synthpop CART, and SMOTE are scheduled under a common budget in predeclare\_v2. This panel scores regression retention.

\textbf{Utility ratios, fidelity, and privacy.} Retention is a null-normalized TSTR/TRTR ratio; Esteban, Hyland, and R{\"a}tsch introduced train-on-synthetic, test-on-real and train-on-real, test-on-real~\citep{esteban2017rcgan}. Utility reviews separate marginal fidelity from downstream prediction~\citep{snoke2018utility,raab2018practical}; alpha-precision, beta-recall, SDMetrics, and synthcity are the usual fidelity reports~\citep{alaa2022faithful,zhang2020sdmetrics,qian2023synthcity}. Differentially private Bayesian networks and marginal synthesizers optimize a privacy budget~\citep{zhang2014privbayes,mckenna2021mst,mckenna2022aim,cai2021privmrf}. Membership inference, shadow-model attacks, and holdout-referenced distances form the empirical privacy literature~\citep{shokri2017membership,hayes2018logan,vanbreugel2023domias,stadler2022groundhog,platzer2021holdout}; formal differential privacy is a different claim from an empirical resemblance score~\citep{dwork2014foundations,abadi2016deep}.

\textbf{Auditors and rank tests.} Tree models remain strong tabular predictors, which is why CatBoost is one of the three auditors~\citep{grinsztajn2022trees,prokhorenkova2018catboost,shwartz-ziv2022tabular}. TabZilla and TabArena show how brittle a single split can be~\citep{mcelfresh2023tabzilla,erickson2026tabarena}. The regression lineages come from PMLB~\citep{olson2017pmlb,romano2021pmlb}. Comparing several methods on the same lineages needs a rank test and a critical difference, or a Bayesian signed-rank alternative~\citep{demsar2006statistical,benavoli2017time}.
