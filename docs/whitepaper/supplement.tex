Real fit rows, real holdout rows, and synthetic rows share one map. The
map is fit on the real fit rows only. Numeric columns go to the open unit
interval by an empirical mid-rank quantile. Categorical levels are
pre-encoded to fixed codes in that same interval. Column names become HMAC
digests. The map withholds the minimum and the maximum. A second fit on
the synthetic rows is a protocol failure. The tabular auditors and the
embedding distance both see this mapped pair.

Representation closeness asks whether Kumo large places the mapped
synthetic table near the mapped real table. The distance is half the
Euclidean length between the two unit-length vectors, and it lies in
$[0,1]$. Closeness is one minus that distance divided by the distance to a
matched null, clamped to $[0,1]$. The synthetic table must also sit closer than an
independent-marginal table of the same size, under the same map. The
matched null is selected from three independent-marginal tables drawn on
the shared map. The seed of each table is the first eight bytes,
little-endian, of the SHA digest of the dataset, the method, the
configuration, the sample seed, and the candidate index. The candidate
indices are $0$, $1$, and $2$. The loss is ordinary linear mean squared
error on the mapped holdout. Candidates are ordered by loss, then by candidate
index; the middle candidate is the matched null. Those losses are fixed before any encoder runs.
Mitra and TabICL must show the same sign as Kumo large on the gap against
the independent-marginal table. A sign flip, a zero match
distance, or a missing vector makes the component null. The component is
required. It stays separate from the tabular transfer.

The headline encoder is Kumo large. Its loss is the structured-data-model
regressor's mean of the inverse-transformed quantile forecasts. A single
in-context prediction is a different scale and stays outside this loss. Mitra and
TabICL repeat that transfer on the same mapped tables. Both are required
for the tabular gate. Neither is averaged into representation closeness.
Mitra is the zero-shot regressor. TabICL restores the context scale before
the squared error. Kumo small and the project encoder a0 stay unscored.
Kumo is available under the OpenMDW
license and the Apache license. Mitra is Apache-licensed. TabICL is
BSD-licensed. The chase score refuses
Foundation and TabPFN, whose licenses stay outside the clean set for this
release. It also refuses the target encoder, the historical context
assembly, Hyperion, TabDPT, and the target-aware blocks. TabDPT's weights
are Apache-licensed and still refused, because that model was trained on
real tables that overlap this panel. Hyperion is the project's and still
refused, because one training arm saw labels. The $4{,}168$-dimensional
vector remains the router embedding. It stays outside the historical formula.
The shipped generator remains \texttt{features12\_\allowbreak{}steps2048}.

The object a recipient keeps is that generator together with a hash
manifest: a salt identifier, the normalizer name, the encoder pin, one
digest per column, a dtype code, and the byte length. The manifest carries
no row, header, category string, extremum, or encoder weight. The salt and
the hash-to-label map stay in a local file the recipient holds. A public
sidecar that echoes a source string or an extremum fails the cleartext
gate. An exact copy of a real row fails its own gate. The near-copy floor
is the $0.01$ quantile, with linear interpolation, of squared Euclidean
distances from each distinct real fit row to the nearest other distinct
real fit row on the shared map. A synthetic row whose squared distance
falls below that floor fails the gate. An undefined floor fails the gate.
Crossing $10{,}240$ bytes fails the byte gate. In each case the scalar
stays null.

The tabular error and the embedding distance answer different questions.
Tabular transfer asks whether a clean tabular model conditioned on the
synthetic rows recovers the improvement that the same model obtained from
real fit rows. Representation closeness asks whether the mapped synthetic
table sits near the mapped real table. A release needs both measurements.
A copied row, a cleartext string, or a byte-cap failure still removes the
score.

\section{Related work}
\paragraph{Generators, by family.}
Parametric copulas~\citep{patki2016sdv} and Chow--Liu trees~\citep{chow1968dependence}
are the classical density models.
Conditional GANs and variational autoencoders, including CTGAN, TVAE, TableGAN,
CTAB-GAN, and CTAB-GAN+, are a second family~\citep{xu2019ctgan,park2018tablegan,zhao2021ctabgan,zhao2024ctabganplus}.
Diffusion and score-based models are a third family. TabDDPM and TabSyn sit
in that family with STASy, CoDi, TabDiff, CDTD, Forest-Diffusion, and
GATD~\citep{kotelnikov2023tabddpm,zhang2024tabsyn,kim2023stasy,lee2023codi,shi2025tabdiff,mueller2025cdtd,jolicoeurmartineau2024forestdiffusion,zagardo2026gatd}.
Autoregressive and language-model generators, and prior-data fitted
generators, are a fourth family~\citep{borisov2023great,solatorio2023realtabformer,ma2023tabpfgen,hollmann2025tabpfn}.
Graphical neural generators are separate from those four~\citep{liu2023goggle}.
ARF estimates density with a forest~\citep{watson2023arf}.
CART synthesis is the synthpop line~\citep{nowok2016synthpop}.
A survey collects these design choices~\citep{borisov2024survey}.
SMOTE is an oversampling method and stays unrun~\citep{chawla2002smote}.
A parametric copula optimizes a joint density; the encoding differs because the shipped object is the byte-budgeted residual and the release gate stays closed on a retention.
A Chow--Liu tree optimizes a tree-structured dependence model; the encoding differs because the artifact is the residual the encoding writes, under that byte budget and release gate.
A conditional GAN optimizes an adversarial match to the training rows; the encoding differs because it ships that residual and keeps the release score null until the gates pass.
A VAE optimizes reconstruction and a latent regularizer; the encoding differs because its artifact is the same byte-budgeted residual, with the release gate separate from the latent objective.
A diffusion model optimizes a denoising or score objective; the encoding differs because the displayed generator is the byte-budgeted residual, and the release gate stays a separate null score.
An autoregressive language-model generator optimizes serialized-row likelihood; the encoding differs because it stores a screened residual of fixed width and a release gate.
A forest density model, including ARF, optimizes axis-aligned splits of the training table; the encoding differs because the shipped file is the byte-budgeted residual and the release score stays null.
CART synthesis optimizes sequential conditional draws; the encoding differs because the artifact is that residual under the byte budget, with the release gate left closed.
A prior-data fitted network optimizes a pretraining objective on other tables; the encoding differs because this paper's artifact is fit on the lineage under the byte budget, and the release score stays null.
A differentially private synthesizer optimizes a privacy-budget constraint; the encoding differs because its release gate is the MFS contract, and an empirical distance stays outside differential privacy.

\paragraph{What this paper measured.}

\section{Delta-method variance of retention}
\label{app:retention-variance}
Let $D = L_{\mathrm{null}} - L_{\mathrm{real}}$ and write the retention ratio as
$R = (L_{\mathrm{null}} - L_{\mathrm{synthetic}}) / D$ when $D \neq 0$.
The partial derivatives are
\begin{align*}
\frac{\partial R}{\partial L_{\mathrm{null}}}
  &= \frac{L_{\mathrm{synthetic}} - L_{\mathrm{real}}}{D^{2}}, \\
\frac{\partial R}{\partial L_{\mathrm{synthetic}}}
  &= -\frac{1}{D}, \\
\frac{\partial R}{\partial L_{\mathrm{real}}}
  &= \frac{R}{D}.
\end{align*}
If the three losses have covariance $\Sigma$, the delta-method variance of
this one lineage's retention is $\nabla R^{\top} \Sigma \nabla R$.
The inverse powers of $D$ in this gradient allow the variance to diverge
as $D$ approaches zero. The informativeness rule therefore drops a lineage
whose real improvement is zero or smaller than $0.01$ times the absolute
null loss. The rule is a stability cut. A published standard error stays outside this rule.
This appendix states the expansion. A numerical
covariance stays unplugged, and the lineage-bootstrap intervals in the tables stay in place.
