\begin{abstract}
We introduce \textbf{D}ownstream \textbf{O}bjective-\textbf{P}reserving \textbf{E}ncoding (DOPE), the byte-budgeted tabular encoding and the generator that encoding writes.
Retention, the shared utility column, is a null-normalized train-on-synthetic/train-on-real ratio~\citep{esteban2017rcgan}. A retention leaves the release gate closed.
On $\NDopeCb$ informative PMLB regression lineages, fit seed $11$ and synthetic size $4n$, the CatBoost median retention is $\MedDopeCb$ (95\% lineage-bootstrap interval $\LoDopeCb$ to $\HiDopeCb$).
The strongest full-panel baseline is author-default ARF. The DOPE generator's retention minus ARF has CatBoost paired median $\DiffArfCb$, with family-cluster bootstrap interval $\LoDiffArfCb$ to $\HiDiffArfCb$ ($n=\NDiffArfCb$; \WtlArfCb).
The linear ARF interval is $\LoDiffArfLin$ to $\HiDiffArfLin$ and contains zero.
Inside the density block the strongest tuned baseline is the Gaussian copula. Its linear interval is $\LoDiffLinGauss$ to $\HiDiffLinGauss$ and contains zero. Equivalence stays unclaimed.
Forest-Flow leads on its $\NForestCb$-lineage block, and that Holm test stays non-significant.
Kumo large, Mitra, and TabICL stay unpooled from CatBoost.
MFS-v2 and MFS-v3 stay null. Official tests stay sealed.
The distance ledger stays outside differential privacy and outside HIPAA de-identification.
The $10{,}240$-byte cap is a release gate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\vspace{-.1cm}

A generator paper often reports the metric that also selected the
configuration. Those native scores live on different scales: predictive
efficacy for CTGAN and TVAE~\citep{xu2019ctgan}, held-out log density for a
Gaussian copula~\citep{patki2016sdv}, a forest density for
ARF~\citep{watson2023arf}, and a diffusion or CART objective for other
families~\citep{kotelnikov2023tabddpm,nowok2016synthpop}. This paper separates
selection from the common measurement. \cref{tab:selection} states the rule,
the rows, and the budget for each method. CTGAN and TVAE are selected on the
same validation rows and the same linear and MLP auditors that later score
them. Chow--Liu and the independent marginals report no search. After those
choices, the shared columns are retention and the bytes charged to the
artifact. Retention is the null-normalized ratio in \eqref{eq:retention}.
The object under test is the encoding. The ship decision is the release
gate in \cref{sec:kpi}.

\subsection{Downstream objective-preserving encoding}
\label{sec:intro-definition}

\vspace{-.1cm}

DOPE is the byte-budgeted encoding in \cref{sec:method} and the generator
that encoding writes. Figure legends label that generator DOPE. Retention
records how much of a real-trained regressor's improvement over a null is
recovered by an auditor trained on synthetic rows. That ratio is the
TSTR/TRTR comparison named in \cref{sec:related}. The decision those
columns can support is narrow. Retention is the measurement this paper can
finish. A best-generator ranking and a ship decision both stay outside
that column. Master Fitness
v2 (MFS-v2) and MFS-v3, defined in \cref{sec:kpi}, are the release
scores. Both are null on every method here. A missing component or a
failed gate removes the scalar rather than publishing a small number.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Displayed generator}
The displayed profile is the generator this paper reports, and it is the
DOPE artifact named in the abstract and in the figure legends. Its configuration
id is \texttt{features12\_\allowbreak{}steps2048}: at most 12 screened inputs,
a hidden width of 16, a $\tanh$ activation, 2{,}048 AdamW steps at learning
rate $0.002$, a refit readout, and no embedding. It is a compact neural
residual. It stays outside the GAN, diffusion, and transformer families.
\cref{fig:arch} is the pipeline. Training screens the inputs, maps them
through the width-16 $\tanh$ layer, and runs AdamW on the regression
mean-squared error, with gradient clipping at $5$. The hidden weights and
biases are then frozen.
The intercept, the linear skip, and the hidden readout are refit on those
frozen features. Coefficients whose absolute value is below $10^{-5}$ are
dropped from the skip. For a selected input vector $x_S$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:residual}
h_j = \tanh\!\left(b_j + w_j^{\top} x_S\right),
\quad
\hat{y} = a + \beta^{\top} x_S + v^{\top} h .
\end{equation}
There is no embedding table in \eqref{eq:residual}. A separate dataset-action
router uses a vector of dimension $856 + 24\times(128+10) = 4{,}168$. That
vector stays unused for the rows in this paper.

Equation~\eqref{eq:residual} is the conditional mean. The joint draw is separate.
Sampling for this profile uses the symbolic kernel stored in the artifact.
The separate neural-joint sampler stays unused. The dependence stored
with an artifact is one choice from the compile search: independence, a
Chow--Liu tree, a triangular autoregression, a sparse Gaussian copula, a
factor model, a truncated vine, or a two-component mixture. The displayed
artifacts span several dependence families.

Training fits the residual. Sampling draws a row from the stored artifact.
Training screens the inputs, ahead of sampling, by absolute rank correlation with
the target and keeps at most 12. The artifact stores a marginal and a
transform for each feature, the selected dependence, the residual, a noise
model, and a per-feature missingness rate. A draw samples a latent vector
from the stored dependence, maps each coordinate through the standard normal
distribution function, inverts the marginal, and inverts the feature
transform. The residual then supplies $\hat{y}$. When the training target falls outside an all-zero or all-one column, the stored noise is heteroscedastic: a linear
fit of the log absolute residual on up to four screened features, with the
scale clamped between the lower and upper deciles of those absolute
residuals. The sampled target adds Gaussian noise of that scale and then
clamps the sum to the unit interval of the training transform. The returned
target stays on that unit interval. Each feature is then
replaced, independently, by a missing mark with its stored probability. For
the displayed DOPE artifacts, the charged byte count is the encoded model
file plus \texttt{projection.json}. The publisher adds those two lengths.
A baseline charge is the length stored for that method. This paper keeps that stored length. Before that encoding, the compiler quantizes the marginals, the
dependence, the residual, and the noise model.

The profile was fixed before the shared retention was inspected. Compile-time
search records the proxy $0.45u + 0.25d + 0.30j$ and then keeps the candidate
with the smallest normalized gate shortfall, then the smallest artifact, then
the lowest candidate id. Every candidate record withholds a release
certificate. The compile record stays outside a release certificate. No global DOPE
family has been selected. The production default, used for a profile other than
this research profile, clamps the hidden width from \ProductionWidthLo\ to
\ProductionWidthHi\ and trains for \ProductionSteps\ steps. That default stays
outside this result. Other shapes stay outside this measurement: micro-TVAEs, TinyMAT,
joint TVAE, a masked transformer, and a TabSyn candidate. A direct-rank
TabDDPM candidate is a research comparator with no claim on the
10{,}240-byte tier. The scored runs leave the per-step loss unstored. A
later logged replay stores one. The supplement plots that replay. The
replay files stay distinct from the scored artifacts, and a lower
hidden-basis loss at 8{,}192 steps leaves the displayed profile in place.
The full width-and-step grid is \FullAblationGrid.

\subsection{A fixed encoding of the columns}
\label{sec:encoding}
The displayed generator and the dataset encoding both reduce a table to a
fixed set of columns. The budgets differ, and both budgets belong to the
current method.

Training screens columns by absolute rank correlation with the target and
keeps at most 12. Those columns are the support $x_S$ in
\eqref{eq:residual}. The artifact stores their positions in the training
table. That screen is what keeps the residual inside the byte cap. It is a
weak dataset key. A column left unranked by the target is dropped, and a
permutation of the remaining columns changes the stored positions.

The encoding an index can store is the sketch the router already consumes.
\texttt{embed-dataset} min-max normalizes each training column onto the unit
interval, leaves a missing entry missing, and leaves the official
test unread. Each column becomes twelve content summaries: a missing rate, moments,
quantiles, a unique-value share, a tail ratio, and the absolute association
with the target. Columns are sorted by those summaries, variance first, then by the
rest of the summary, then by the sorted observations. A permutation of columns or of rows
leaves the vector fixed. The width of that sketch is
$10 + 12\times 6 + 64\times 12 + 6 = 856$.
The ten-number header records the task, the log row count, the log column
count, and the shape of the target. Each of the twelve content summaries is
then reduced across columns to a six-number distribution: the minimum, the
lower quartile, the median, the upper quartile, the maximum, and the mean.
That is the $12\times 6$ block. The sketch then keeps the first 64 columns
in the order above and stores each column's twelve summaries, padding a
shorter table with zeros. Absolute correlations among the kept columns
collapse to one further six-number distribution, so the width stays independent of
the square of the column count.

The $4{,}168$-dimensional vector appends 24 candidate states from one router
bundle onto that sketch. A second bundle moves the same table. An index
stores the 856-wide sketch. The longer vector is a route from one bundle.

A neighbor found with the sketch is still fit with the displayed
profile. The artifact is still charged in bytes, and it still faces the
release score in \cref{sec:kpi}.
The sketch stays outside the fitness components. A router action that names a
retention or a failure probability is a prediction from the bundle. It stays
outside \eqref{eq:mfs}.

\subsection{Retention}
\label{sec:retention}
Let $L_{\mathrm{null}}$, $L_{\mathrm{real}}$, and
$L_{\mathrm{synthetic}}$ be the auditor's loss on held-out validation rows
when that auditor is trained on a null baseline, on real fit rows, and on
synthetic rows. Those held-out rows come from the official training file.
Retention is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:retention}
\frac{L_{\mathrm{null}}-L_{\mathrm{synthetic}}}{L_{\mathrm{null}}-L_{\mathrm{real}}} .
\end{equation}
Rearrangement, with $r$ the retention, is algebra on those three losses:
\[
L_{\mathrm{synthetic}}
= L_{\mathrm{real}} + (1-r)\bigl(L_{\mathrm{null}}-L_{\mathrm{real}}\bigr).
\]
\RetentionAlgebra\
Equation~\eqref{eq:retention} is a null-normalized TSTR/TRTR
ratio~\citep{esteban2017rcgan}. $L_{\mathrm{synthetic}}$ is the loss of an
auditor trained on synthetic rows and scored on real holdout rows.
$L_{\mathrm{real}}$ is the loss of the same auditor trained on real rows and
scored on those holdout rows. $L_{\mathrm{null}}$ is the null loss on the
holdout. The encoding and the byte-budgeted release gate are the claims.
The ratio is the column they are scored on. Its delta-method variance, and
the reason a small denominator is omitted, is \cref{app:retention-variance}.
A value of $1$ means the synthetic-trained auditor matched the real-trained
auditor. A value above $1$ means it beat that auditor. A negative value means
it did worse than the null. A lineage is informative when the real
improvement is at least $0.01$ times the absolute null loss and stays strictly positive.
Otherwise the lineage is omitted. An omitted lineage stays out of the median. The three
auditors are CatBoost~\citep{prokhorenkova2018catboost}, ordinary linear
regression, and an MLP. The lineage score is the median of sample seeds
$101$, $211$, and $307$. The panel score is the median of those lineage
scores. Intervals below are percentile lineage-bootstrap intervals,
10{,}000 resamples of lineages, with the three seeds kept inside the lineage
median. Each bar is that lineage interval.

\subsection{The release score}
\label{sec:kpi}
The decision this project is built to make is whether an artifact may ship.
Retention is the measurement this paper can finish. It is the shared column
after the choices in \cref{tab:selection}. A high retention
means an auditor trained on synthetic rows recovered the improvement that
the same auditor obtained from real fit rows. That can be true of an
artifact that is too large to ship, that misses the joint distribution,
that copies training rows, or that moves the features a later model relies
on. The release score keeps those failures apart from a
utility median.

MFS-v2 is that score. With component $c_i\in[0,1]$, weight $w_i>0$,
$\epsilon=10^{-6}$, and $W=\sum_i w_i$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mfs}
\mathrm{MFS\text{-}v2}
= 100\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{W}\sum_i w_i\ln(\epsilon+c_i)\right)
\end{equation}
when the hard-gate map is non-empty, every hard gate passed, and every
positive-weight component is finite and inside $[0,1]$. Otherwise the scalar
is null. The mean is geometric. A component near zero pulls the product
down. A component that is missing, or that falls outside $[0,1]$, removes
the score rather than publishing a small number. An empty gate map, or any
failed hard gate, does the same. Privacy has soft weight zero. A membership
failure, an attribute failure, or a copied row is a gate. Utility cannot
offset it. The weights are utility transfer $0.30$, driver fidelity $0.20$,
distribution fidelity $0.20$, structure fidelity $0.15$, coverage realism
$0.10$, and compactness $0.05$.

The six components ask six different questions.
Utility transfer is a one-sided lower bound on retention, clamped into
$[0,1]$. It is the bound inside \eqref{eq:mfs}, and it is distinct from the
median of \eqref{eq:retention} in the figures below.
Driver fidelity is an equal mixture of feature-importance rank agreement and
top-feature-set overlap between the real table and the synthetic table.
Distribution fidelity is half the minimum marginal fidelity, plus a quarter
of the sliced-Wasserstein fidelity and a quarter of the maximum mean
discrepancy fidelity.
Structure fidelity is the minimum dependence fidelity.
Coverage realism, on a fixed embedding of holdout rows, is $0.25$ precision,
$0.25$ recall, $0.20$ density capped at $1$, and $0.30$ coverage.
Compactness is $\mathrm{clamp}(1 - \mathrm{bytes}/10240,\, 0,\, 1)$. An
artifact past $10{,}240$ bytes fails the byte gate, and the scalar stays
null. Crossing the cap is a failed gate, distinct from a retention of zero.

MFS-v3 is the score a later release has to pass. MFS-v2 is unchanged.
Historical receipts still pin the v2 file. The v3 weights add one
component to the geometric mean. Utility transfer is $0.35$.
Representation closeness, driver fidelity, and distribution fidelity are
each $0.15$. Structure fidelity is $0.10$. Coverage realism and
compactness are each $0.05$. Privacy stays at soft weight zero. A missing
component, a failed gate, a missing prerequisite, or any other weight
vector makes the scalar null. Each component enters as $\max(c_i,\epsilon)$.
With $W=\sum_i w_i$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mfsv3}
\mathrm{MFS\text{-}v3}
= \mathrm{clip}_{[0,100]}\!\left(
100\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{W}\sum_i w_i\ln(\max(c_i,\epsilon))\right)
\right)
\end{equation}
when the four prerequisites hold, every hard gate passed, and every
positive-weight component is finite and inside $[0,1]$. Otherwise the
scalar is null. A profile of all ones is $100$. \eqref{eq:mfs} adds
$\epsilon$ inside the logarithm, so the same all-ones profile illustrates
as $\MfsAllOnes$, above $100$. That illustration is arithmetic on the
historical formula. The supplement's generated rows use \eqref{eq:mfsv3}.
The four prerequisites are a complete real-versus-real control, the
required size cells, verified artifact-only sampling, and locked metric
implementations. The Python scorer implements this clip. The native Rust
evaluator still adds $\epsilon$ inside the logarithm and still omits these
prerequisites, so the two implementations remain short of parity.

Distribution fidelity, structure fidelity, coverage realism, and
compactness keep their v2 definitions. The driver term stored beside
this panel is the same equal mixture of rank agreement and top-feature-set
overlap, applied to absolute correlations with the target. It stays distinct from the
permutation-importance auditor in the v2 definition, and this paper
withholds it as a component value. v2 utility transfer
remains the one-sided lower bound on
retention for the CatBoost, linear, and MLP auditors in the tables below.
v3 utility transfer is that same bound on the Kumo large retention.
The model is conditioned on the synthetic rows and scored
on real holdout rows. Mitra and TabICL must be measured on the same mapped
tables for the gate. Their retentions stay out of that bound.
A CatBoost retention cannot fill this component.

Real fit rows, real holdout rows, and synthetic rows share one map. The
map is fit on the real fit rows only. Numeric columns go to the open unit
interval by an empirical mid-rank quantile. Categorical levels are
pre-encoded to fixed codes in that same interval. Column names become HMAC
digests. The map withholds the minimum and the maximum. A second fit on
the synthetic rows is a protocol failure. The tabular auditors and the
embedding distance both see this mapped pair.

Representation closeness asks whether Kumo large places the mapped
synthetic table near the mapped real table. The distance is half the
Euclidean length between the two unit-length vectors, and it lies in
$[0,1]$. Closeness is one minus that distance divided by the distance to a
matched null. The synthetic table must also sit closer than an
independent-marginal table of the same size, under the same map. The
matched null is the median of three independent-marginal tables drawn on
the shared map. The seed of each table is the first eight bytes,
little-endian, of the SHA digest of the dataset, the method, the
configuration, the sample seed, and the candidate index. The candidate
indices are $0$, $1$, and $2$. The loss is ordinary linear mean squared
error on the mapped holdout. The median loss selects the table, and a tie
keeps the lower index. Those losses are fixed before any encoder runs.
Mitra and TabICL must show the same sign as Kumo large on the gap against
the independent-marginal table. A sign flip, a zero match
distance, or a missing vector makes the component null. The component is
required. It stays separate from the tabular transfer.

The headline encoder is Kumo large. Its loss is the structured-data-model
regressor's mean of the inverse-transformed quantile forecasts. A single
in-context prediction is a different scale and stays outside this loss. Mitra and
TabICL repeat that transfer on the same mapped tables. Both are required
for the tabular gate. Neither is averaged into representation closeness.
Mitra is the zero-shot regressor. TabICL restores the context scale before
the squared error. Kumo small and the project encoder a0 stay unscored.
Kumo is available under the OpenMDW
license and the Apache license. Mitra is Apache-licensed. TabICL is
BSD-licensed. The chase score refuses
Foundation and TabPFN, whose licenses stay outside the clean set for this
release. It also refuses the target encoder, the historical context
assembly, Hyperion, TabDPT, and the target-aware blocks. TabDPT's weights
are Apache-licensed and still refused, because that model was trained on
real tables that overlap this panel. Hyperion is the project's and still
refused, because one training arm saw labels. The $4{,}168$-dimensional
vector remains the router embedding. It stays outside \eqref{eq:mfs}.
The shipped generator remains \texttt{features12\_\allowbreak{}steps2048}.

The object a recipient keeps is that generator together with a hash
manifest: a salt identifier, the normalizer name, the encoder pin, one
digest per column, a dtype code, and the byte length. The manifest carries
no row, header, category string, extremum, or encoder weight. The salt and
the hash-to-label map stay in a local file the recipient holds. A public
sidecar that echoes a source string or an extremum fails the cleartext
gate. An exact copy of a real row fails its own gate. The near-copy floor
is the $0.01$ quantile, with linear interpolation, of squared Euclidean
distances from each distinct real fit row to the nearest other distinct
real fit row on the shared map. A synthetic row whose squared distance
falls below that floor fails the gate. An undefined floor fails the gate.
Crossing $10{,}240$ bytes fails the byte gate. In each case the scalar
stays null.

The tabular error and the embedding distance answer different questions.
Tabular transfer asks whether a clean tabular model conditioned on the
synthetic rows recovers the improvement that the same model obtained from
real fit rows. Representation closeness asks whether the mapped synthetic
table sits near the mapped real table. A release needs both measurements.
A copied row, a cleartext string, or a byte-cap failure still removes the
score.

PTF-v1 is the other release KPI. Its hard gates include joint fidelity,
membership and attribute attacks, copy checks, and feature-importance
agreement on a sealed holdout. It is null here, as are MFS-v2 and MFS-v3.
Compile-time
search records the proxy $0.45u + 0.25d + 0.30j$ and then keeps the
candidate with the smallest gate shortfall, then the smallest artifact.
That record withholds a release certificate. The proxy stays outside
\eqref{eq:mfs} and \eqref{eq:mfsv3}.

No artifact in this paper has a measured MFS-v2 or MFS-v3 value. Emitting
MFS-v2 requires the six components, a non-empty hard-gate map that passed,
both the size-$n$ and size-$4n$ artifacts, real-versus-real controls,
artifact-only sampling, and locked metric implementations. Emitting MFS-v3
requires \eqref{eq:mfsv3}: the four prerequisites named above, the clean

\subsection{Statistics}
\label{sec:stats}
Comparisons are paired on the lineages where both methods have a complete
informative group. The tests are two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the
nonzero paired differences, and one-sided sign tests for a positive
difference, with a matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation.
Holm correction is applied inside a family, separately for each test, and is
stays inside its own family. On CatBoost the Holm sign-test values are
$\SignHolmGaussCb$, $\SignHolmChowCb$, and $\SignHolmIndCb$.
The density family has nine tests (three comparators times three auditors)
and applies to the density rows of \cref{tab:density,tab:headline}.
The neural family has six tests (CTGAN and TVAE times the three auditors)
and stays unpooled from the density family.
The Forest-Flow family has three tests (one comparator times the three
auditors) on $\NForestCb$ lineages and stays unpooled from either family.
\cref{tab:mfsv3} is outside those families. Its Holm adjustment is one
family per auditor, each of size three: that auditor against the three
density comparators. A rejection stays inside its own family.
In a W/T/L count, the tie is a paired difference of zero. A
non-significant Wilcoxon test stays distinct from that tie, and equivalence stays unclaimed.
Equivalence would require a two one-sided test against a margin declared
before the test. This paper leaves that test unreported.
A Friedman test and a Nemenyi
critical difference use the four density methods on their common
complete-informative set, with rank 1 assigned to the higher retention. The
critical-difference constant for four methods at $\alpha=0.05$ is $2.569$.

\section{Delta-method variance of retention}
\label{app:retention-variance}
Let $D = L_{\mathrm{null}} - L_{\mathrm{real}}$ and write \eqref{eq:retention} as
$R = (L_{\mathrm{null}} - L_{\mathrm{synthetic}}) / D$ when $D \neq 0$.
The partial derivatives are
\begin{align*}
\frac{\partial R}{\partial L_{\mathrm{null}}}
  &= \frac{L_{\mathrm{synthetic}} - L_{\mathrm{real}}}{D^{2}}, \\
\frac{\partial R}{\partial L_{\mathrm{synthetic}}}
  &= -\frac{1}{D}, \\
\frac{\partial R}{\partial L_{\mathrm{real}}}
  &= \frac{R}{D}.
\end{align*}
If the three losses have covariance $\Sigma$, the delta-method variance of
this one lineage's retention is $\nabla R^{\top} \Sigma \nabla R$.
As $D$ approaches zero the gradient grows like $1/D$ and $1/D^{2}$, so that
variance is unbounded. The informativeness rule therefore drops a lineage
whose real improvement is zero or smaller than $0.01$ times the absolute
null loss. The rule is a stability cut. A published standard error stays outside this rule.
This appendix states the expansion. A numerical
covariance stays unplugged, and the lineage-bootstrap intervals in the tables stay in place.
