\begin{abstract}
This paper measures how much of a real-trained regressor's improvement
over a null is recovered by an auditor trained on synthetic rows. The
generator is evaluated against a 10{,}240-byte artifact budget. It is a compact residual,
not a GAN, a diffusion model, or a transformer: at most 12 screened
inputs, a width-16 $\tanh$ layer, a refit readout, and 2{,}048 AdamW
steps at learning rate $0.002$, with no embedding. The configuration id
is \texttt{features12\_\allowbreak{}steps2048}. The panel is $\PreparedN$
prepared PMLB regression lineages, including simulated physics and
simulated dynamics, with a grouped 80/20 cut of the official training
rows, seed $\SplitSeed$, fit seed 11, and synthetic size $4n$. On
$\NDopeCb$ informative lineages the CatBoost median retention is
$\MedDopeCb$ (95\% lineage-bootstrap interval $\LoDopeCb$ to
$\HiDopeCb$). Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon tests separate this generator from
a Gaussian copula, a Chow--Liu tree, and independent marginals. Against
the copula the record is \WtlGaussCb\ wins, ties, and losses. The linear
auditor does not separate the two (\WtlLinGauss). That interval contains
zero, and the result is not an equivalence test. Unpooled blocks compare
CTGAN and TVAE on $\NNeuCb$ lineages and Forest-Flow on $\NForestCb$
lineages (\WtlForestCb; $\ForestByteLo$ to $\ForestByteHi$ bytes).
$\DopeWithinCap$ artifacts are inside the cap, and two are over it.
Official tests were not opened. TabSyn is \TabSynResult, the 100-lineage
TabDDPM publication is \TabDDPMResult, and the full Forest-Flow
publication is \ForestPublication. Fit-seed variance is \FitSeedVariance.
Release scores are null. The distance ledger is not differential privacy
or HIPAA de-identification.

\section{Introduction}
A generator paper often reports the metric that also selected the
configuration. Those native scores live on different scales: predictive
efficacy for CTGAN and TVAE~\cite{xu2019ctgan}, held-out log density for a
Gaussian copula~\cite{patki2016sdv}, a forest density for
ARF~\cite{watson2023arf}, and a diffusion or CART objective for other
families~\cite{kotelnikov2023tabddpm,nowok2016synthpop}. This paper separates
selection from the common measurement. After each method has been selected
on its own objective, the shared columns are retention and the bytes charged
to the artifact.

The decision those columns can support is narrow. It is whether a network
small enough to ship under a 10{,}240-byte cap still preserves downstream
regression utility on held-out validation rows. It is not a declaration that
one generator is best, and it is not a certification score. Master Fitness
v2 (MFS-v2), a gated geometric score, is defined in Section~\ref{sec:kpi}.
It is null on every method here, because the six components and the hard-gate
map are not all present.

The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The displayed residual and the generative procedure that samples a row
from that artifact (Section~\ref{sec:method}, Fig.~\ref{fig:arch},
\eqref{eq:residual}). That procedure is not the production default and it
does not use the $4{,}168$-dimensional router.
\item Retention and charged bytes on the validation panel, with
lineage-bootstrap intervals, and the algebra of a retention value
(Fig.~\ref{fig:bars}, Fig.~\ref{fig:bytes}).
\item Paired tests inside unpooled blocks: the density block, the neural
block, and the $\NForestCb$-lineage Forest-Flow confirmation, including the
linear non-separation and the Forest-Flow lead (Fig.~\ref{fig:pairs}).
\end{enumerate}
\paragraph{Scope.}
\label{sec:scope}
TabSyn is \TabSynResult. The 100-lineage TabDDPM publication is
\TabDDPMResult. The full Forest-Flow publication is \ForestPublication.
The pre-specified privacy-attack panel is \PrivacyAttackPanel. Fit-seed
variance is \FitSeedVariance. The full ablation grid is \FullAblationGrid.
The primary displayed DOPE and legacy comparison fits use seed 11 and
coresets of \CoreMin\ to \CoreMax\ rows. Later retained ARF and
Forest-Flow panels report their additional fit seeds separately.
Official tests stay sealed. MFS-v2 is null. PTF-v1, the release KPI whose
hard gates include joint fidelity, membership and attribute attacks, copy
checks, and feature-importance agreement on a sealed holdout, is null. A release at the
10{,}240-byte tier is a separate claim from the byte cap, and it is not
made here. BeyondArena, a separate family-fit check outside this PMLB

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
Tabular synthesis has moved from parametric copulas~\cite{patki2016sdv} and
Chow--Liu trees~\cite{chow1968dependence} through conditional GANs, including
TableGAN, and variational autoencoders~\cite{xu2019ctgan,park2018tablegan} to diffusion and score-based
models~\cite{kotelnikov2023tabddpm,kim2023stasy,lee2023codi,zhang2024tabsyn,shi2025tabdiff,mueller2025cdtd,jolicoeurmartineau2024forestdiffusion,zagardo2026gatd}.
Autoregressive and language-model generators~\cite{borisov2023great,solatorio2023realtabformer},
prior-data fitted generators~\cite{ma2023tabpfgen,hollmann2025tabpfn},
CTAB-GAN and CTAB-GAN+~\cite{zhao2021ctabgan,zhao2024ctabganplus}, and
graphical neural generators~\cite{liu2023goggle} target the same mixed-type
tables. ARF estimates density with a forest~\cite{watson2023arf}. CART
synthesis is the synthpop line~\cite{nowok2016synthpop}. A recent survey
collects these design choices~\cite{borisov2024survey}. This paper runs the
Gaussian copula, a Chow--Liu baseline written for this comparison,
independent marginals written for this comparison, CTGAN, TVAE,
and a $\NForestCb$-lineage Forest-Flow confirmation. New retained panels
also measure ARF and a scaled author-default TabSyn subset
(Section~\ref{sec:retained}). TabDDPM on the
$\PreparedN$-lineage panel, STaSy, CoDi, TabDiff, CTAB-GAN+, GReaT,
REaLTabFormer, and TabPFGen are cited and not measured. SMOTE~\cite{chawla2002smote} was not run.
This panel scores regression retention, not classification oversampling.

Differentially private Bayesian networks and marginal
synthesizers~\cite{zhang2014privbayes,mckenna2021mst,mckenna2022aim,cai2021privmrf} optimize
a privacy budget. Membership inference and shadow-model attacks are a
separate detection literature~\cite{shokri2017membership,hayes2018logan,vanbreugel2023domias}.
Formal differential privacy is a different claim from an empirical
resemblance score~\cite{dwork2014foundations,abadi2016deep}. Utility reviews
separate marginal fidelity from downstream
prediction~\cite{snoke2018utility,raab2018practical}. Alpha-precision,
beta-recall, SDMetrics, and synthcity are the usual fidelity
reports~\cite{alaa2022faithful,zhang2020sdmetrics,qian2023synthcity}.
The fidelity results add alpha-precision, beta-recall, a
distance-to-closest-record share, and a DOMIAS AUC. ARF and the retained
TabSyn subset have an additional fidelity, detection and empirical-privacy
panel in Section~\ref{sec:retained}. The displayed-profile ARF
publication median is \ArfResult.
The pre-specified privacy-attack panel
remains \PrivacyAttackPanel. Nothing in those fields is a formal privacy
guarantee or a HIPAA de-identification claim.

Tree models remain strong tabular predictors, which is why CatBoost is one
of the three auditors~\cite{grinsztajn2022trees,prokhorenkova2018catboost,shwartz-ziv2022tabular}.
TabZilla and TabArena show how brittle a single split can
be~\cite{mcelfresh2023tabzilla,erickson2026tabarena}. The regression
lineages come from PMLB~\cite{olson2017pmlb,romano2021pmlb}. Comparing
several methods on the same lineages needs a rank test and a critical
difference, or a Bayesian signed-rank alternative~\cite{demsar2006statistical,benavoli2017time}.
This paper reports the frequentist procedure: a Friedman test and a Nemenyi
critical difference on the common set, plus paired Wilcoxon tests. The
Bayesian signed-rank procedure is not computed.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Displayed generator}
The displayed profile is the generator this paper reports. Its configuration
id is \texttt{features12\_\allowbreak{}steps2048}: at most 12 screened inputs,
a hidden width of 16, a $\tanh$ activation, 2{,}048 AdamW steps at learning
rate $0.002$, a refit readout, and no embedding. It is a compact neural
residual, not a GAN, a diffusion model, or a transformer.
Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} is the pipeline. Training screens the inputs, maps them
through the width-16 $\tanh$ layer, and runs AdamW on the regression
mean-squared error, with gradient clipping at $5$. The hidden weights and
biases are then frozen.
The intercept, the linear skip, and the hidden readout are refit on those
frozen features. Coefficients whose absolute value is below $10^{-5}$ are
dropped from the skip. For a selected input vector $x_S$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:residual}
h_j = \tanh\!\left(b_j + w_j^{\top} x_S\right),
\quad
\hat{y} = a + \beta^{\top} x_S + v^{\top} h .
\end{equation}
There is no embedding table in \eqref{eq:residual}. A separate dataset-action
router uses a vector of dimension $856 + 24\times(128+10) = 4{,}168$. That
vector does not generate the rows in this paper.

Equation~\eqref{eq:residual} is the conditional mean, not the joint draw.
Sampling for this profile uses the symbolic kernel stored in the artifact.
It does not use the separate neural-joint sampler. The dependence stored
with an artifact is one choice from the compile search: independence, a
Chow--Liu tree, a triangular autoregression, a sparse Gaussian copula, a
factor model, a truncated vine, or a two-component mixture. This paper does
not treat the displayed artifacts as one dependence family.

Training fits the residual. Sampling draws a row from the stored artifact.
Training, not sampling, screens inputs by absolute rank correlation with
the target and keeps at most 12. The artifact stores a marginal and a
transform for each feature, the selected dependence, the residual, a noise
model, and a per-feature missingness rate. A draw samples a latent vector
from the stored dependence, maps each coordinate through the standard normal
distribution function, inverts the marginal, and inverts the feature
transform. The residual then supplies $\hat{y}$. When the training target is
not entirely zeros and ones, the stored noise is heteroscedastic: a linear
fit of the log absolute residual on up to four screened features, with the
scale clamped between the lower and upper deciles of those absolute
residuals. The sampled target adds Gaussian noise of that scale and then
clamps the sum to the unit interval of the training transform. It is not
returned in the original units of the source column. Each feature is then
replaced, independently, by a missing mark with its stored probability. The
charged byte count is the length of the encoded artifact. Before that
encoding, the compiler quantizes the marginals, the dependence, the
residual, and the noise model.

The profile was fixed before the shared retention was inspected. Compile-time
search records the proxy $0.45u + 0.25d + 0.30j$ and then keeps the candidate
with the smallest normalized gate shortfall, then the smallest artifact, then
the lowest candidate id. Every candidate record withholds a release
certificate. The compile record is not a release certificate. No global DOPE
family has been selected. The production default, used when this research
profile is not selected, clamps the hidden width from \ProductionWidthLo\ to
\ProductionWidthHi\ and trains for \ProductionSteps\ steps. It is not this
result. Other shapes exist and are not this measurement: micro-TVAEs, TinyMAT,
joint TVAE, a masked transformer, and a TabSyn candidate. A direct-rank
TabDDPM candidate is a research comparator with no claim on the
10{,}240-byte tier. The scored runs did not store a per-step loss. A
later logged replay did. The supplement plots that replay. The
replay files are not substituted for the scored artifacts, and a lower
hidden-basis loss at 8{,}192 steps does not replace the displayed profile.
The full width-and-step grid is \FullAblationGrid.

\subsection{A fixed encoding of the columns}
\label{sec:encoding}
The displayed generator and the dataset encoding both reduce a table to a
fixed set of columns. The budgets differ, and both budgets belong to the
current method.

Training screens columns by absolute rank correlation with the target and
keeps at most 12. Those columns are the support $x_S$ in
\eqref{eq:residual}. The artifact stores their positions in the training
table. That screen is what keeps the residual inside the byte cap. It is a
weak dataset key. A column the target does not rank is dropped, and a
permutation of the remaining columns changes the stored positions.

The encoding an index can store is the sketch the router already consumes.
\texttt{embed-dataset} min-max normalizes each training column onto the unit
interval, leaves a missing entry missing, and does not read the official
test. Each column becomes twelve content summaries: a missing rate, moments,
quantiles, a unique-value share, a tail ratio, and the absolute association
with the target. Columns are sorted by those summaries, variance first, then by the
rest of the summary, then by the sorted observations. A permutation of columns or of rows does
not move the vector. The sketch keeps the first 64 columns in that order and
pads a shorter table with zeros. Absolute correlations among the kept
columns collapse to one six-number distribution, so the width does not grow
with the square of the column count. A ten-number header records the task,
the log row count, the log column count, and the shape of the target. The
width of that sketch is 856.

The $4{,}168$-dimensional vector appends 24 candidate states from one router
bundle onto that sketch. A second bundle moves the same table. An index
stores the 856-wide sketch. The longer vector is a route from one bundle.

A neighbor found with the sketch is still fit with the displayed
profile. The artifact is still charged in bytes, and it still faces the
release score in Section~\ref{sec:kpi}.
The sketch is not a seventh fitness component. A router action that names a
retention or a failure probability is a prediction from the bundle. It does
not enter \eqref{eq:mfs}.

\subsection{Retention}
Let $L_{\mathrm{null}}$, $L_{\mathrm{real}}$, and
$L_{\mathrm{synthetic}}$ be the auditor's loss on held-out validation rows
when that auditor is trained on a null baseline, on real fit rows, and on
synthetic rows. Those held-out rows come from the official training file.
Retention is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:retention}
\frac{L_{\mathrm{null}}-L_{\mathrm{synthetic}}}{L_{\mathrm{null}}-L_{\mathrm{real}}} .
\end{equation}
Rearrangement, with $r$ the retention, is algebra on those three losses:
\[
L_{\mathrm{synthetic}}
= L_{\mathrm{real}} + (1-r)\bigl(L_{\mathrm{null}}-L_{\mathrm{real}}\bigr).
\]
\RetentionAlgebra\
A value of $1$ means the synthetic-trained auditor matched the real-trained
auditor. A value above $1$ means it beat that auditor. A negative value means
it did worse than the null. A lineage is informative when the real
improvement is at least $0.01$ times the absolute null loss and is not zero.
Otherwise the lineage is omitted. It is not entered as zero. The three
auditors are CatBoost~\cite{prokhorenkova2018catboost}, ordinary linear
regression, and an MLP. The lineage score is the median of sample seeds
$101$, $211$, and $307$. The panel score is the median of those lineage
scores. Intervals below are percentile lineage-bootstrap intervals,
10{,}000 resamples of lineages, with the three seeds kept inside the lineage
median. They are not per-seed whiskers.

\subsection{The release score}
\label{sec:kpi}
The decision this project is built to make is whether an artifact may ship.
Retention is the measurement this paper can finish. It is the shared column
after each method has been selected on its own objective. A high retention
means an auditor trained on synthetic rows recovered the improvement that
the same auditor obtained from real fit rows. That can be true of an
artifact that is too large to ship, that misses the joint distribution,
that copies training rows, or that moves the features a later model relies
on. The release score exists so those failures are not traded against a
utility median.

MFS-v2 is that score. With component $c_i\in[0,1]$, weight $w_i>0$,
$\epsilon=10^{-6}$, and $W=\sum_i w_i$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mfs}
\mathrm{MFS\text{-}v2}
= 100\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{W}\sum_i w_i\ln(\epsilon+c_i)\right)
\end{equation}
when the hard-gate map is non-empty, every hard gate passed, and every
positive-weight component is finite and inside $[0,1]$. Otherwise the scalar
is null. The mean is geometric. A component near zero pulls the product
down. A component that is missing, or that falls outside $[0,1]$, removes
the score rather than publishing a small number. An empty gate map, or any
failed hard gate, does the same. Privacy has soft weight zero. A membership
failure, an attribute failure, or a copied row is a gate. Utility cannot
offset it. The weights are utility transfer $0.30$, driver fidelity $0.20$,
distribution fidelity $0.20$, structure fidelity $0.15$, coverage realism
$0.10$, and compactness $0.05$.

The six components ask six different questions.
Utility transfer is a one-sided lower bound on retention, clamped into
$[0,1]$. It is the bound inside \eqref{eq:mfs}, and it is distinct from the
median of \eqref{eq:retention} in the figures below.
Driver fidelity is an equal mixture of feature-importance rank agreement and
top-feature-set overlap between the real table and the synthetic table.
Distribution fidelity is half the minimum marginal fidelity, plus a quarter
of the sliced-Wasserstein fidelity and a quarter of the maximum mean
discrepancy fidelity.
Structure fidelity is the minimum dependence fidelity.
Coverage realism, on a fixed embedding of holdout rows, is $0.25$ precision,
$0.25$ recall, $0.20$ density capped at $1$, and $0.30$ coverage.
Compactness is $\mathrm{clamp}(1 - \mathrm{bytes}/10240,\, 0,\, 1)$. An
artifact past $10{,}240$ bytes fails the byte gate, and the scalar stays
null. Crossing the cap is a failed gate, distinct from a retention of zero.

MFS-v3 is the score a later release has to pass. MFS-v2 is unchanged.
Historical receipts still pin the v2 file. \eqref{eq:mfs} is the same
geometric mean, with one added weight. Utility transfer is $0.35$.
Representation closeness, driver fidelity, and distribution fidelity are
each $0.15$. Structure fidelity is $0.10$. Coverage realism and
compactness are each $0.05$. Privacy stays at soft weight zero. A missing
component, a failed gate, or any other weight vector makes the scalar
null.

Distribution fidelity, structure fidelity, coverage realism, and
compactness keep their v2 definitions. The driver term stored beside
this panel is rank agreement of absolute correlations with the target.
It is not the permutation-importance auditor in the v2 definition, and
this paper does not publish it as a component value. v2 utility transfer
remains the one-sided lower bound on
retention for the CatBoost, linear, and MLP auditors in the tables below.
v3 utility transfer is that same bound when the auditor is a clean-license
tabular model. The model is conditioned on the synthetic rows and scored
on real holdout rows. Kumo large is the headline auditor. Mitra and TabICL
repeat the measurement on the same mapped tables. A CatBoost retention
cannot fill this component.

Real fit rows, real holdout rows, and synthetic rows share one map. The
map is fit on the real fit rows only. Numeric columns go to the open unit
interval by an empirical mid-rank quantile. Categorical levels are
pre-encoded to fixed codes in that same interval. Column names become HMAC
digests. The map does not publish a minimum or a maximum. A second fit on
the synthetic rows is a protocol failure. The tabular auditors and the
embedding distance both see this mapped pair.

Representation closeness asks whether Kumo large places the mapped
synthetic table near the mapped real table. The distance is half the
Euclidean length between the two unit-length vectors, and it lies in
$[0,1]$. Closeness is one minus that distance divided by the distance to a
matched null. The synthetic table must also sit closer than an
independent-marginal table of the same size, under the same map. The
matched null is a second table whose cheap-model scores sit in a band
fixed before the synthetic rows are encoded. Mitra and TabICL must show
the same sign as Kumo large on that gap. A sign flip, a zero match
distance, or a missing vector makes the component null. The component is
required. It does not stand in for the tabular transfer.

The headline encoder is Kumo large. Its loss is the structured-data-model
regressor's mean of the inverse-transformed quantile forecasts. A single
in-context prediction is a different scale and is not this loss. Mitra and
TabICL repeat that transfer on the same mapped tables. Both are required
for the tabular gate. Neither is averaged into representation closeness.
Mitra is the zero-shot regressor. TabICL restores the context scale before
the squared error. Kumo small and the project encoder a0 were not run.
Kumo is available under the OpenMDW
license and the Apache license. Mitra is Apache-licensed. TabICL is
BSD-licensed. The chase score refuses
Foundation and TabPFN, whose licenses are not in the clean set for this
release. It also refuses the target encoder, the historical context
assembly, Hyperion, TabDPT, and the target-aware blocks. TabDPT's weights
are Apache-licensed and still refused, because that model was trained on
real tables that overlap this panel. Hyperion is the project's and still
refused, because one training arm saw labels. The $4{,}168$-dimensional
vector remains the router embedding. It is not an input to \eqref{eq:mfs}.
The shipped generator remains \texttt{features12\_\allowbreak{}steps2048}.

The object a recipient keeps is that generator together with a hash
manifest: a salt identifier, the normalizer name, the encoder pin, one
digest per column, a dtype code, and the byte length. The manifest carries
no row, header, category string, extremum, or encoder weight. The salt and
the hash-to-label map stay in a local file the recipient holds. A public
sidecar that echoes a source string or an extremum fails the cleartext
gate. An exact copy of a real row fails its own gate. Crossing
$10{,}240$ bytes fails the byte gate. In each case the scalar stays null.

The tabular error and the embedding distance answer different questions.
Tabular transfer asks whether a clean tabular model conditioned on the
synthetic rows recovers the improvement that the same model obtained from
real fit rows. Representation closeness asks whether the mapped synthetic
table sits near the mapped real table. A release needs both measurements.
A copied row, a cleartext string, or a byte-cap failure still removes the
score.

PTF-v1 is the other release KPI. Its hard gates include joint fidelity,
membership and attribute attacks, copy checks, and feature-importance
agreement on a sealed holdout. It is null here, as are MFS-v2 and MFS-v3.
Compile-time
search records the proxy $0.45u + 0.25d + 0.30j$ and then keeps the
candidate with the smallest gate shortfall, then the smallest artifact.
That record withholds a release certificate. The proxy is not
\eqref{eq:mfs}.

No artifact in this paper has a measured MFS-v2 or MFS-v3 value. Emitting
MFS-v2 requires the six components, a non-empty hard-gate map that passed,
both the size-$n$ and size-$4n$ artifacts, real-versus-real controls,
artifact-only sampling, and locked metric implementations. Emitting MFS-v3
also requires the clean tabular transfer and the embedding distance under
the shared map, and every hard gate has to pass. Table~\ref{tab:mfsv3}
reports the measured retentions. A retention is not the scalar. The
cleartext scan is unscanned where the stored column codes are internal
identifiers, and other gates fail on many lineages, so the scalar stays
null. A retention win does not supply those inputs. Copying a retention number into \eqref{eq:mfs} would publish a value
the contract refuses to emit. The supplement applies \eqref{eq:mfs} to
invented components, including a zero compactness term. Those rows are
arithmetic. They show the shape of the product. They are not a score for any
method in the tables. Baselines stay on their native objectives. The shared
columns in this paper stay retention and charged bytes.

\subsection{Statistics}
Comparisons are paired on the lineages where both methods have a complete
informative group. The tests are two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the
nonzero paired differences, and one-sided sign tests for a positive
difference, with a matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation.
Holm correction is applied inside a family, separately for each test, and is
not pooled across families. On CatBoost the Holm sign-test values are
$\SignHolmGaussCb$, $\SignHolmChowCb$, and $\SignHolmIndCb$.
The density family has nine tests (three comparators times three auditors).
The neural family is CTGAN and TVAE times the auditors. The Forest-Flow
family is separate and has six lineages. A Friedman test and a Nemenyi
critical difference use the four density methods on their common
complete-informative set, with rank 1 assigned to the higher retention. The
critical-difference constant for four methods at $\alpha=0.05$ is $2.569$.
The primary comparison interval resamples informative lineages. Each lineage
contributes the median of sample seeds $101$, $211$, and $307$ from the
single fit at seed $11$. Later ARF and Forest-Flow panels report between-fit
variation separately. This primary interval does not include a new fit, a new
split, a new population, or a change of auditor.
These are descriptive validation tests. They are not the release KPI in
Section~\ref{sec:scope}, and they do not authorize a release at the
10{,}240-byte tier. MFS-v2 and PTF-v1 stay null.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}
\paragraph{Internal.}
The primary displayed DOPE and legacy comparison fits use the coreset
in Section~\ref{sec:setup} and fit seed $11$,
and a training-derived validation cut of the official training rows. The
scored retention panel is regression only. Other tasks are not a result
of these tables. The
supplement records the draw. The official test was not an input to
training, selection, or the numbers in this paper. The informativeness rule
drops a negligible real-versus-null gap. \ThrSentence\ Incomplete groups
are omitted, not zeroed. Chow--Liu and independent marginals are
implementations written for this comparison. CTGAN and TVAE use the
lineages that finished. Runs that stopped at the recorded time limit are
omitted. They are not counted as failures of a finished fit.

The supplementary budget protocol retains the frozen \BaselineForestFitLimit-second Forest-Flow
fit cap and all timed-out attempt receipts. Timeout cells have missing quality
metrics and confer no DOPE win; coverage denominators include their recorded
dispositions. The October 8 disposition addendum was written after \BaselineForestTimeouts\ observed
timeouts and is not retroactive preregistration. A longer-budget sensitivity
track would require a separate prospective freeze and comparable method
budgets, with both tracks' costs retained. The supplement's
\path{BASELINE_PROTOCOL.md} links the frozen completion checkpoint and
every timeout disposition.

The validation cut also supplies native tuning signals. These are
post-selection validation estimates, not independent official-test
estimates. Lineage intervals hold the split and fitted artifact fixed;
they omit fitting uncertainty. Related simulation lineages can violate
the independence assumption behind both bootstrap and order-statistic
intervals. The retained-subset results therefore remain exploratory.

\paragraph{External.}
The primary displayed DOPE fits do not establish behavior on the full
tables used by the diffusion papers, across five fit seeds, or on the
official test. The additional ARF and Forest-Flow fit seeds do not establish
DOPE fitting uncertainty.
Generalization past seed $\SplitSeed$ is \FitSeedVariance. Forest-Flow's
byte comparison uses $\NForestCb$ lineages and the hash-checked fit files.
BeyondArena is a separate fit check. It is not a retention panel, and it is
not pooled with the density block.

\paragraph{Construct.}
Retention is the reported utility. Both release scores in
Section~\ref{sec:kpi} stay null. The linear auditor does not separate DOPE from the Gaussian copula,
and that result is not an equivalence. Shipping this artifact is not the
same experiment as shipping the compact predictor itself. The panel does
not contain that control, so it does not show that a recipient needs the
synthetic rows rather than the predictor. Normalized-space RMSE is the loss
the auditors stored. Original-unit error after inverting the training
transform was not stored, and this paper does not report it.
Table~\ref{tab:fidelity} is not a membership-inference certification. Its
unbalanced DCR share and DOMIAS AUC are empirical. Empirical resemblance is not
differential privacy and is not HIPAA de-identification.

\paragraph{Statistical.}
The rank tests are descriptive, not the pre-registered production
superiority test. Density, neural, Forest-Flow, and BeyondArena stay
unpooled. A small retention $p$-value does not flip the null scalar.

\section{Conclusion}
On $\NDopeCb$ regression lineages and fit seed $11$, generator
\texttt{features12\_\allowbreak{}steps2048} has
CatBoost retention $\MedDopeCb$ (interval $\LoDopeCb$ to $\HiDopeCb$)
and $\DopeWithinCap$ artifacts inside the cap. CatBoost separates it
from the density comparators. The linear auditor does not separate it
from the Gaussian copula, and that result is not an equivalence. The
$\NForestCb$-lineage Forest-Flow block leads on CatBoost (\WtlForestCb)
and the MLP (\WtlForestMlp). Those counts are not a certified win. The
unmeasured publications stay unmeasured. The Kumo large, Mitra, and
TabICL retentions are in Table~\ref{tab:mfsv3}. MFS-v2 and MFS-v3 stay
null, as do the scores in Section~\ref{sec:scope}. Official tests stay sealed.
