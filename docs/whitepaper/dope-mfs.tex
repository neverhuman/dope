\begin{abstract}
Synthetic tables ship as rows or as multi-megabyte neural generators, often scored by the metric that selected them.
We introduce \textbf{D}ownstream \textbf{O}bjective-\textbf{P}reserving \textbf{E}ncoding (DOPE), which compiles a numeric regression table into a generator under a $10{,}240$-byte release cap (median $\VDopeBytes$ bytes; $\VDopeWithin$ fits within it): quantized marginals, a dependence model, a twelve-input neural residual for the target, and a noise model; sampling reads only the artifact.
We score retention, a null-normalized train-on-synthetic/train-on-real ratio~\citep{esteban2017rcgan}, under three auditors on $\PreparedN$ PMLB regression tables with paired, family-clustered intervals.
DOPE's profile was chosen at one fit seed; we refit at five.
Under CatBoost at synthetic size $4n$, DOPE's median retention is $\VDopeCbFour$ [$\VDopeCbFourLo$, $\VDopeCbFourHi$].
DOPE \VVerdictStrong\ \VStrongestName, the strongest full-panel generator (Hodges--Lehmann difference $\VStrongDiff$ [$\VStrongLo$, $\VStrongHi$]), whose $\VStrongBytes$ median artifact is $\VStrongRatio$ times DOPE's, and \VVerdictArfCbFour\ author-default ARF ($\VDiffArfCbFour$ [$\VLoArfCbFour$, $\VHiArfCbFour$]).
On a separate $\VNForestCbFour$-lineage block, DOPE \VVerdictForestCbFour\ Forest-Flow ($\VDiffForestCbFour$ [$\VLoForestCbFour$, $\VHiForestCbFour$]).
It \VVerdictPredCbFour\ a predictor-only sampler ($\VDiffPredCbFour$ [$\VLoPredCbFour$, $\VHiPredCbFour$]) and \VVerdictBootCbFour\ four-fold resampled real rows ($\VDiffBootCbFour$ [$\VLoBootCbFour$, $\VHiBootCbFour$]).
All estimates use a validation cut; the official test split is reserved for one pre-registered run.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A synthetic table is useful when a model trained on it behaves like a model trained on the real table. Two practices make that property hard to compare across generators. First, released generators are large: CTGAN and TVAE~\citep{xu2019ctgan}, TabDDPM~\citep{kotelnikov2023tabddpm}, TabSyn~\citep{zhang2024tabsyn}, and forest flows~\citep{jolicoeurmartineau2024forestdiffusion} ship hundreds of kilobytes to gigabytes of weights, which are expensive to audit, to store next to a dataset, and to reason about as a release. Second, a generator paper often reports the metric that also chose its configuration, so selection and measurement are entangled.

DOPE starts from the downstream task. When the intended use is regression on a target $y$, a generator only has to reproduce the feature distribution well enough and the conditional mean $\mathbb{E}[y\mid x]$ well enough for a learner to recover it. DOPE therefore stores the conditional mean explicitly, as a tiny residual network over a screened set of inputs, and stores the features as quantized marginals tied together by a compact dependence model. The whole artifact must fit a byte budget, and sampling reads nothing but the artifact.

We evaluate with one measurement for every method. Retention~\eqref{eq:retention} asks how much of a real-trained auditor's improvement over a null an auditor recovers when trained on synthetic rows. Two controls separate the ingredients: resampling real rows four-fold isolates the effect of row count, and a predictor-only sampler, which keeps a fitted regressor but drops the joint feature model, isolates the dependence model. Every comparison is paired by lineage, uses five DOPE fit seeds, and carries a nested bootstrap interval with simulated source families as clusters. Each method's selection data are stated.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{A kilobyte generator compiled from the table.} DOPE stores per-feature marginals and transforms, a dependence model chosen among seven families on the training rows, a twelve-input residual for the target, and a heteroscedastic noise model, all quantized into an artifact whose median charge is $\VDopeBytes$ bytes (\cref{sec:method}, \cref{alg:dope}).
  \item \textbf{A selection-aware, controlled measurement.} Retention is credited as a TSTR/TRTR ratio and computed by three auditors at sizes $n$ and $4n$, beside a row-count control and a predictor-only control, with pre-declared Holm families and a robust equivalence rule (\cref{sec:protocol}).
  \item \textbf{Where DOPE stands, and why.} Under CatBoost at $4n$, DOPE \VVerdictStrong\ \VStrongestName\ while storing $\VStrongRatio$ times fewer bytes, \VVerdictArfCbFour\ author-default ARF, and \VVerdictPredCbFour\ the predictor-only control; resampled real rows remain the upper reference (\cref{sec:results}).
  \item \textbf{A release gate that fails closed.} MFS-v3 is a gated geometric mean with an executable conformance test. It is null for every method here, and the paper names the gates that keep it null (\cref{sec:gate}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Generators.} Copula and Bayesian-network models~\citep{patki2016sdv,chow1968dependence,aas2009paircopula,zhang2014privbayes} fit explicit, often small, joint models; marginal-based private synthesizers~\citep{mckenna2021mst,mckenna2022aim,cai2021privmrf} add formal privacy. GANs and VAEs~\citep{xu2019ctgan,park2018tablegan,zhao2021ctabgan}, diffusion and score models~\citep{kotelnikov2023tabddpm,zhang2024tabsyn,kim2023stasy,lee2023codi,shi2025tabdiff}, forest flows~\citep{watson2023arf,jolicoeurmartineau2024forestdiffusion}, language-model generators~\citep{borisov2023great,solatorio2023realtabformer}, sequential CART~\citep{nowok2016synthpop}, and prior-fitted networks~\citep{ma2023tabpfgen,hollmann2025tabpfn} optimize a likelihood, a denoising objective, or adversarial agreement on the joint table. DOPE optimizes a different thing: the target's conditional mean, explicitly, under a byte budget, with the joint feature model kept compact.

\textbf{Compact and program models of tables.} Bayesian synthesis of probabilistic programs~\citep{saad2019bayesian} and CrossCat~\citep{mansinghka2016crosscat} also produce small, inspectable generative models of tables. They target the joint distribution and posterior inference; DOPE targets a downstream regression objective and charges every artifact in bytes against a release cap.

\textbf{Evaluation.} Train-on-synthetic/test-on-real comparisons originate with \citet{esteban2017rcgan}; utility and fidelity measures~\citep{snoke2018utility,raab2018practical,alaa2022faithful,zhang2020sdmetrics} and benchmark suites~\citep{qian2023synthcity,mcelfresh2023tabzilla,erickson2026tabarena} broaden them. Membership attacks on synthetic data~\citep{shokri2017membership,hayes2018logan,vanbreugel2023domias,stadler2022groundhog} and holdout-referenced distances~\citep{platzer2021holdout} assess privacy empirically, which is distinct from differential privacy~\citep{dwork2014foundations}. Our retention is a null-normalized TSTR/TRTR ratio; the new elements are the controls, the paired multi-seed design, and the byte axis.

\section{DOPE}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{The artifact}
\label{sec:artifact}
A DOPE artifact for a numeric table with features $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$ and target $y$ stores five parts. (i) For every feature, a quantized marginal, a monotone transform onto the unit interval, and a missing-value rate. (ii) A dependence model over the Gaussianized features, chosen among seven families: independence, a Chow--Liu tree, a triangular autoregression, a sparse Gaussian copula, a factor model, a truncated vine, and a two-component mixture. (iii) A residual model for the target on a screened support $x_S$ of at most twelve inputs, ranked by absolute rank correlation with $y$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:residual}
h_j = \tanh\!\left(b_j + w_j^{\top} x_S\right),
\qquad
\hat{y}(x) = a + \beta^{\top} x_S + v^{\top} h ,
\end{equation}
with sixteen hidden units. (iv) A heteroscedastic noise model: a linear fit of $\log|y-\hat{y}|$ on up to four screened features, with its scale clamped between the lower and upper deciles of the absolute residuals. (v) The positions of the screened columns. The compiler quantizes every part and encodes the artifact; the byte charge is the encoded model file plus the \texttt{projection.json} that maps raw columns onto the unit interval. An artifact larger than $10{,}240$ bytes fails the release gate.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{DOPE compile and sample. Training reads the fit rows only; sampling reads the artifact only.}
\label{alg:dope}
\small
\textbf{Compile}$(D_{\mathrm{fit}}, B)$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=1pt]
  \item Fit each feature's marginal and transform $T_j$ and record its missing rate $m_j$.
  \item Keep the twelve features with the largest absolute rank correlation with $y$; call them $S$.
  \item Train \eqref{eq:residual} with AdamW (learning rate $0.002$, clipped gradients) for the profile's step budget; freeze $(b,W)$ and refit $(a,\beta,v)$ in closed form.
  \item Fit the noise model on the residuals $y-\hat{y}(x)$.
  \item For each dependence family, fit it on the Gaussianized features, quantize, encode, and record a proxy score and the gate shortfall.
  \item Return the candidate with the smallest gate shortfall, then the fewest bytes; an artifact above the budget $B$ fails the release gate.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Sample}$(A, N)$: for each of $N$ rows, draw $z$ from the stored dependence; set $x_j = T_j^{-1}\!\big(F_j^{-1}(\Phi(z_j))\big)$; set $y = \mathrm{clamp}_{[0,1]}\big(\hat{y}(x_S) + \sigma(x)\,\varepsilon\big)$ with $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$; replace each feature by a missing mark with probability $m_j$.
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Compile, sample, and profile}
\label{sec:compile}
\cref{alg:dope} lists the two procedures. The compile search reads the fit rows only. The reported profile, \texttt{features12\_\allowbreak{}steps2048}, trains for $2{,}048$ steps; the untuned trainer default trains for $\ProductionSteps$ steps with a width between $\ProductionWidthLo$ and $\ProductionWidthHi$. The reported profile was chosen on a six-lineage discovery panel and checked on six disjoint confirmation lineages, by validation retention at fit seed $11$; those twelve lineages belong to the panel below. \cref{sec:seeds} and \cref{tab:headline,tab:sizen} report the untuned default beside it, and the sealed test removes this selection from the final estimate.

\subsection{Release gate}
\label{sec:gate}
Retention alone cannot decide a release: an artifact can score well and still copy rows, miss the joint distribution, or exceed the byte cap. MFS-v3 combines seven components $c_i\in[0,1]$ (utility transfer $0.35$; representation closeness, driver fidelity, and distribution fidelity $0.15$ each; structure fidelity $0.10$; coverage realism and compactness $0.05$ each) as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mfsv3}
\mathrm{MFS\text{-}v3}
= \mathrm{clip}_{[0,100]}\!\left(
100\exp\!\left(\frac{\sum_i w_i\ln(\max(c_i,\epsilon))}{\sum_i w_i}\right)
\right),\quad \epsilon = 10^{-6},
\end{equation}
and returns null unless four prerequisites hold (a real-versus-real control, both size cells, artifact-only sampling, locked metric implementations) and every hard gate passes; membership, attribute, and copy failures are gates, so utility cannot offset them. Compactness is $\mathrm{clamp}(1-\mathrm{bytes}/10240,0,1)$. A conformance test ties \eqref{eq:mfsv3} to the contract and the scorer (supplement~B). The score is null for every method in this paper because its privacy and representation gates are still unmeasured.

\section{Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
\subsection{Data and partitions}
The panel holds the $\PreparedN$ rights-cleared PMLB regression lineages~\citep{olson2017pmlb,romano2021pmlb} that passed the data lock; $\VSimulatedN$ of them come from four simulated families (Feynman, Strogatz, Friedman-style \texttt{fri}, and BNG generators). Large sources are reduced by a fixed coreset to at most $\CoreMax$ fit rows. The official training rows are split $80/20$ by seed $\SplitSeed$ into fit and validation rows, grouping duplicates. The official test rows stay unread until a single pre-registered run; every number here is a validation estimate.

\subsection{Retention}
\label{sec:retention}
Let $L_{\mathrm{null}}$, $L_{\mathrm{real}}$, and $L_{\mathrm{syn}}$ be an auditor's validation mean squared error, on the target min--max projected to $[0,1]$ by the training split, when it predicts the training mean, when it is trained on the real fit rows, and when it is trained on synthetic rows. Retention is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:retention}
R = \frac{L_{\mathrm{null}}-L_{\mathrm{syn}}}{L_{\mathrm{null}}-L_{\mathrm{real}}} ,
\end{equation}
so $R=1$ matches the real-trained auditor and $R<0$ is worse than the null. A lineage is informative for an auditor when $L_{\mathrm{null}}-L_{\mathrm{real}}\geq 0.01\,|L_{\mathrm{null}}|$. The auditors are CatBoost~\citep{prokhorenkova2018catboost}, ordinary least squares, and a one-hidden-layer MLP; CatBoost and the MLP use seed $1729$. A synthetic table has $n$ or $4n$ rows, where $n$ is the fit-row count, and three sample seeds. A fit's value is the median over sample seeds; a lineage's value is the median over its fits.

\subsection{Comparators and controls}
\label{sec:selection}
Comparators are author-default and native-selected ARF~\citep{watson2023arf} at five fit seeds; TabSyn with a scaled schedule of $200$ VAE and $1{,}000$ diffusion epochs; Gaussian copula, Chow--Liu, and independent marginals selected by held-out log density; CTGAN and TVAE selected on validation skill of the linear and MLP auditors; and a historical Forest-Flow block. Supplement~C lists each method's selection rule, data, and budget. The \emph{predictor-only} control fits a ridge regression on the fit rows, samples each feature independently from its marginal, and adds Gaussian residual noise; it keeps a regressor and drops the joint model. The \emph{real-row} control resamples the fit rows with replacement to $4n$ rows; it measures what extra rows alone do and is an upper reference rather than a generator.

\subsection{Statistics}
\label{sec:stats}
Comparisons pair lineages that both arms complete (a five-seed arm needs three complete fits). The paired estimand is the Hodges--Lehmann pseudo-median of the lineage differences, the location the Wilcoxon signed-rank test addresses. Intervals come from a nested bootstrap with $10{,}000$ draws: clusters (each simulated family is one cluster; every other lineage is its own), then lineages inside a drawn cluster, then fit seeds inside a drawn lineage, separately for each arm. Wilcoxon $p$-values are Holm-adjusted inside the pre-declared families of each size (\cref{tab:headline}). The Wilcoxon test treats lineages as independent; each verdict reads the clustered interval. A $90\%$ interval inside $\pm\VMargin$ would establish equivalence. \VEquivSentence\ These choices were committed before the five-seed refit ran, but after an earlier four-seed run whose samples the refit reproduces at seeds $23$ to $71$ (\cref{sec:seeds}).

\subsection{What the controls show}
\label{sec:controls}
The predictor-only control keeps a fitted ridge regressor and drops the joint feature model, so its gap bundles DOPE's dependence model with DOPE's nonlinear residual. Under CatBoost, DOPE minus predictor-only is $\VDiffPredCbFour$ ($\VLoPredCbFour$ to $\VHiPredCbFour$); under the MLP it is $\VDiffPredMlpFour$ and under the linear auditor $\VDiffPredLinFour$. A tree or network auditor trained on independently drawn features sees feature combinations that the real table never contains, while a linear auditor depends mostly on the regressor. The real-row control resamples real rows to $4n$; DOPE minus that control is $\VDiffBootCbFour$ ($\VLoBootCbFour$ to $\VHiBootCbFour$) under CatBoost and $\VDiffBootMlpFour$ under the MLP, so DOPE \VVerdictBootCbFour\ resampled real rows under CatBoost. Extra rows lift an MLP auditor even when they are copies, which is why MLP retentions above one appear.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:threats}
\textbf{Validation estimates.} Validation rows informed DOPE's profile (by retention), the CTGAN and TVAE configurations (by auditor skill), and the native-selected density and ARF configurations (by held-out log density); the sealed test run is the only estimate free of that selection. \textbf{Row count and the regressor.} Retention at $4n$ mixes fidelity with row count, which the real-row control bounds, and the predictor-only gap credits DOPE's dependence model and its nonlinear residual together. \textbf{Scope.} The panel is regression only, fit views hold at most $\CoreMax$ rows, and $\VSimulatedN$ of $\PreparedN$ lineages are simulated; clustering by family widens the intervals but cannot add independent sources. \textbf{Baselines.} TabSyn runs a scaled schedule of $200$ VAE and $1{,}000$ diffusion epochs against the authors' $4{,}000$ and $10{,}001$, TVAE and CTGAN cover small cohorts, and Forest-Flow is a historical block of $\VNForestCbFour$ lineages. The pre-declared five-seed TabSyn and density runs, synthpop, SMOTE, Forest-Flow under the common budget, TabDDPM, TabDiff, and an LLM generator are pending. \textbf{Seeds.} TabSyn, the density baselines, CTGAN, and TVAE contribute one fit seed each, so their side of each interval omits fitting variance. A DOPE fit seed acts on every lineage at once, so the five seeds are five draws of a panel-wide effect, and the nested interval, which resamples seeds within each lineage, does not widen for that shared effect. \textbf{Privacy.} The privacy rows are empirical attacks on released rows; attacks on the artifact itself and shadow-model attacks are pending.

\section{Conclusion}
DOPE compiles a regression table into a generator whose median charge is $\VDopeBytes$ bytes. Under CatBoost at $4n$ it retains $\VDopeCbFour$ of a real-trained auditor's gain, \VVerdictStrong\ \VStrongestName\ at about $\VStrongRatio$ times fewer bytes, and \VVerdictPredCbFour\ a predictor-only sampler. The release score is null until its privacy and representation gates are measured, and the official test split waits for one pre-registered run.
