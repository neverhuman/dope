\section{Scoped prior art}
The comparisons below are alternatives and historical anchors.
This draft does not rank them.

Conditional generative adversarial networks and variational autoencoders for tables are the historical neural anchors \citep{xu-ctgan}.
Tabular diffusion of the Kotelnikov, Baranchuk, Rubachev, and Babenko construction is a neural generator with its own training recipe \citep{kotelnikov-tabddpm}.
Adversarial random forests are a structured generator aimed at density and sampling \citep{watson-arf}.
Dependence trees \citep{chow-liu} and pair-copula constructions \citep{aas-paircopula} are circuit baselines: they encode a dependence program rather than a large neural sample.
synthpop is a tree-based synthesizer and is not treated as an interchangeable joint generator \citep{nowok-synthpop}.
CatBoost is one example of a downstream learner, not a definition of utility \citep{prokhorenkova-catboost}.

The Penn Machine Learning Benchmarks collection is a historical source pool \citep{olson-pmlb,romano-pmlb}.
Renaming its splits does not create an untouched family.
TabSyn, TabDiff, and Forest-Flow are candidates named for later adapter work.
This skeleton does not score them.

A direct predictor, a real-row coreset, and distilled prototypes are alternatives to spending the byte constraint on a generator.
They are specified as controls in the next sections, not as unscored leaderboard rows.
Stadler, Oprisanu, and Troncoso show that a synthetic release is not de-identification \citep{stadler-groundhog}.
That citation limits what this draft is allowed to say.
It is not a result about the compiler.
