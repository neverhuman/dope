Figure~\ref{fig:hero} and Table~\ref{tab:ranking} rank the methods on the development report splits. Of \VThree{Scheduled} scheduled cells, \VThree{Uninformative} lack sufficient predictive signal in the real reference and are excluded for every method under the same rule (\S\ref{sec:protocol}). The remaining \VThree{CandCells} cells cover \VThree{Families} families. Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:paired} and~\ref{tab:repeat-b} report all three sets of splits separately.
Every generator was fitted on the same rows $A$ of each cell and scored by the same auditors on the same test rows; Appendix~\ref{app:arms} gives the third-party configurations.
The scheduled headline cells draw from \ReportPanelValue{scheduled_tables} tables and include different row splits and feature views. The median cell has \ReportPanelValue{features_med} features, \ReportPanelValue{fit_rows_med} fitting rows and \ReportPanelValue{test_rows_med} test rows.

\paragraph{Ranking.}
At \VThree{PrimaryMult}$\times$ sampling, DOPE reaches a Transfer Score of \VThree{CandScore}.
ARF follows at \VThree{ArfScore}, then the Gaussian copula at \VThree{GcScore}, TVAE at \VThree{TvaeScore} and CTGAN at \VThree{CtganScore}.
For reference, a copy of the fitting rows scores \VThree{CopyAScorePoint} and a bootstrap resample of them \VThree{BootAScorePoint}.
A second empirical calibration reference, drawn from disjoint real calibration rows, scores \VThree{TwinCScorePoint} on the \VThree{TwinCCells} informative cells where it is available. Its coverage is narrower than the main table's.
Permuting every column independently scores \VThree{IndepScorePoint} and permuting only the target \VThree{LabelShuffleScorePoint}; CTGAN scores below both.

\paragraph{Paired differences.}
DOPE is ahead of the Gaussian copula by \VThree{GcVsPts} points, of TVAE by \VThree{TvaeVsPts} and of CTGAN by \VThree{CtganVsPts}; each interval is above zero.
Against ARF the difference is \VThree{ArfVsPts} points, and its interval contains zero: on this look DOPE is not shown to transfer better than ARF.
The task-level point estimates show different strengths.
ARF is higher on regression (\VThree{ArfRegressionScore} against \VThree{CandRegressionScore}); DOPE is higher on binary (\VThree{CandBinaryScore} against \VThree{ArfBinaryScore}) and multiclass targets (\VThree{CandMulticlassScore} against \VThree{ArfMulticlassScore}).
The held-out MLP also favors ARF (TRG \VThree{ArfMlp} against \VThree{CandMlp}). This auditor enters the guard only.

\paragraph{Matched size.}
With a synthetic sample the size of $A$ ($m=\VThree{MatchedMult}$), the order is unchanged: DOPE \VThree{CandMatchedScorePoint}, ARF \VThree{ArfMatchedScorePoint}, Gaussian copula \VThree{GcMatchedScorePoint}, TVAE \VThree{TvaeMatchedScorePoint} and CTGAN \VThree{CtganMatchedScorePoint}.
The difference from ARF is \VThree{ArfVsPtsMatched} points.

\paragraph{Size.}
Every DOPE file is within the budget: the median is \VThree{CandBytesMed}~bytes and the largest \VThree{CandBytesMax}.
Across scheduled headline cells, the median ratio of raw fitting-matrix bytes to DOPE artifact bytes is \ReportPanelDecimal{matrix_per_artifact_med}$\times$. The reference counts the target column and the matrix header in a lossless single-precision representation.
The uncapped serialized Python objects have median sizes from \VThree{GcBytesMed}~bytes (Gaussian copula) to \VThree{ArfBytesMed}~bytes (ARF), \VThree{MethodsRatioMin} to \VThree{MethodsRatioMax} times DOPE's median. Every observed file exceeds the cap. These totals include serialization and library state and measure the files actually used for sampling.
Applying the cap to their emitted files would assign every entry the failure floor (TRG \VThree{FailedValue}, a Transfer Score of \VThree{FailedScore}). This calculation rejects oversize outputs from the observed default fits. A comparison with generators fitted under the same cap would require separate runs.

\paragraph{Equal storage.}
Under the same \VThree{BudgetKiB}\,KiB whole-file cap, DOPE scores higher than storing a uniform subset of the fitting rows and resampling them with replacement (Table~\ref{tab:budget}). Both use the same report splits, sample sizes and auditors. The row reference can retain a median of \CapValue{\RowsBudget}{retained_rows_median} complete rows with their targets. The intervals summarize each method separately. Appendix~\ref{app:budget} describes the byte accounting and shows the full budget sweep.

\paragraph{Realism, privacy and copies.}
Realism coverage is \VThree{CandRealism} for DOPE, lower than ARF's \VThree{ArfRealism} and higher than the Gaussian copula's \VThree{GcRealism}; TVAE and CTGAN pass the realism gate on no entry.
Privacy coverage is \VThree{CandPrivacy} for DOPE; CTGAN is higher at \VThree{CtganPrivacy}, the Gaussian copula and TVAE are lower (\VThree{GcPrivacy} and \VThree{TvaePrivacy}), and ARF passes the privacy gate on few entries (\VThree{ArfPrivacy}).
Only CTGAN's mean exact-copy excess fails the exact-copy guard; the maxima in Table~\ref{tab:ranking}(b) show single entries above the bound for the Gaussian copula as well as CTGAN.
Every generator's rows are easy to tell from real ones: the median C2ST AUCs of DOPE and of independently permuted columns both round to \VThree{CandC2st}, and the medians lie between \VThree{ArfC2st} and \VThree{CtganC2st} for the third-party generators, against \VThree{CopyAC2st} for a copy of the fitting rows.

\paragraph{Failures.}
TVAE fails \VThree{TvaeFailed} of \VThree{TvaeCells} cells, where its sample holds a single class and the entry scores \VThree{FailedValue} by protocol; DOPE and the other generators score every cell.

\paragraph{Repeats on other development splits.}
The lower block of Table~\ref{tab:paired} repeats the comparison on other row splits of the same DEV tables, with the same generator recipes, contract and auditors (\VThree{SecondLookCells} informative cells in \VThree{SecondLookFamilies} families; receipt \texttt{\VThree{SecondLookPrimarySha}}).
It was pre-registered before any third-party generator was scored on these splits, but DOPE's own scores on them were already known (\S\ref{sec:limits}).
DOPE is ahead of the Gaussian copula by \VThree{SecondLookGcVsPts} points, of TVAE by \VThree{SecondLookTvaeVsPts} and of CTGAN by \VThree{SecondLookCtganVsPts}. Each interval is above zero on these other splits as well.
Against ARF the difference is \VThree{SecondLookArfVsPts} points and its interval again contains zero at \VThree{PrimaryMult}$\times$.
At matched size the second look's difference from ARF, \VThree{SecondLookArfVsPtsMatched} points, has an interval above zero, unlike the report look's; the promotion rule and the reading above use \VThree{PrimaryMult}$\times$ only.

On the final set of development splits (Table~\ref{tab:repeat-b}), DOPE scores \HPartBScore{\HPartBDope}{score} at \VThree{PrimaryMult}$\times$ and ARF scores \HPartBScore{\HPartBArf}{score}. Their paired interval again crosses zero. The Gaussian copula, TVAE and CTGAN comparisons each have a positive lower bound, and the Gaussian copula comparison misses the task-level guard.
