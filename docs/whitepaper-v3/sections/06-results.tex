Figure~\ref{fig:hero} and Tables~\ref{tab:arms}, \ref{tab:compare} and~\ref{tab:methods} describe the report look of \S\ref{sec:protocol}: \VThree{Scheduled} scheduled cells, of which \VThree{Uninformative} are uninformative on real data and leave every arm, and \VThree{CandCells} informative cells in \VThree{Families} families (\VThree{RegressionFamilies} regression, \VThree{BinaryFamilies} binary and \VThree{MulticlassFamilies} multiclass).

\paragraph{Headline.}
\cli{auto-v8} reaches a Transfer Score of \VThree{CandScore} points (TRG \VThree{CandTrg}), against \VThree{BaseScore} (TRG \VThree{BaseTrg}) for the release~\VThree{BaseVersion} encoder, a paired gain of \VThree{DeltaPts} points (TRG \VThree{Delta}) with $p=\VThree{PHolm}$ (Holm over $m=\VThree{HolmM}$).
Its median file is \VThree{CandBytesMed}~bytes (\VThree{BaseBytesMed} for the baseline), the largest is \VThree{CandBytesMax}, and it fails \VThree{CandFailed} of \VThree{CandCells} cells.
The gain comes from classification: the paired task differences are \VThree{DeltaBinary} for binary and \VThree{DeltaMulticlass} for multiclass targets, and \VThree{DeltaRegression} for regression, where \cli{auto-v8} is lower on the point estimate (\VThree{CandRegression} against \VThree{BaseRegression}).

\paragraph{Matched size.}
With a synthetic sample the size of $A$ ($m=\VThree{MatchedMult}$), \cli{auto-v8} scores \VThree{CandMatched} against \VThree{BaseMatched}; the paired gain \VThree{MatchedDelta} has one-sided $p=\VThree{MatchedP}$ and its interval contains zero.
At matched size the gain is not resolved; the headline rests on the \VThree{PrimaryMult}$\times$ sample.

\paragraph{Against the other arms.}
The analytic packet used on every table, without selection (\VThree{AnalyticId}), scores \VThree{AnalyticTrg} at a median of \VThree{AnalyticBytesMed}~bytes: better than the baseline on regression (\VThree{AnalyticRegression}) but far worse on multiclass targets (\VThree{AnalyticMulticlass}).
Used alone it is worse than the baseline; within \cli{auto-v8} it is written only where the check of \S\ref{sec:select} prefers it.
The Format~1 \texttt{small} programs of releases \VThree{BaseVersion} and \VThree{OldVersion} score \VThree{PrevSmallTrg} and \VThree{OldSmallTrg} and fail \VThree{PrevSmallFailed} of \VThree{PrevSmallCells} cells, and the V7 packet with untrained decoder weights (\VThree{V7Id}) scores \VThree{V7Trg}.

\paragraph{Third-party generators.}
The look also scores four third-party generators, fitted on the same rows with library defaults and no byte cap: the Gaussian copula, CTGAN and TVAE of SDV~\VThree{SdvVersion} and ARF from arfpy~\VThree{ArfpyVersion} (Appendix~\ref{app:arms}).
Each has a lower TRG than \cli{auto-v8} (Tables~\ref{tab:arms} and~\ref{tab:methods}).
ARF comes closest, at \VThree{ArfTrg} and a paired difference of \VThree{ArfVsDelta} (\VThree{ArfVsOutcome}; Holm over $m=\VThree{MethodsHolmM}$), higher than \cli{auto-v8} on regression (\VThree{ArfRegression} against \VThree{CandRegression}) and lower on binary and multiclass targets, with a median file of \VThree{ArfBytesMed}~bytes.
The Gaussian copula (\VThree{GcVsDelta}), TVAE (\VThree{TvaeVsDelta}) and CTGAN (\VThree{CtganVsDelta}) are REJECT; CTGAN's TRG, \VThree{CtganTrgPoint}, is below that of independently permuted columns, and CTGAN alone fails the exact-copy guard.
No third-party file is within the budget: all four fail the byte guard by construction and score \VThree{FailedValue} at the budget (Table~\ref{tab:arms} note).

\paragraph{Controls.}
Training on $A$ itself (\VThree{CopyAId}) scores \VThree{CopyATrgPoint} rather than zero: its entry value is $1-R_B$, and the minimum over auditors falls below zero whenever the twin trains one auditor better than $A$ does. A bootstrap resample of $A$ scores \VThree{BootATrgPoint}; both are reference points for a generator that only sees $A$.
Permuting each column independently (\VThree{IndepId}) scores \VThree{IndepTrgPoint}, and permuting only the target scores \VThree{LabelShuffleTrgPoint}.
The real-twin control from the calibration rows is applicable on only \VThree{TwinCCells} cells (\VThree{TwinCNotApplicable} lack enough rows).

\paragraph{The decision.}
Conditions (i)--(iii) hold, but \VThree{GuardsFailed} of the \VThree{GuardsTotal} guards fail (Table~\ref{tab:compare}): the regression lower bound (\VThree{GuardTasks} against a threshold of \VThree{TaskCiLowGt}), the held-out MLP (\VThree{GuardMlp}), realism coverage (\VThree{GuardRealism}; \VThree{CandRealism} against \VThree{BaseRealism}) and privacy coverage (\VThree{GuardPrivacy} against a threshold of \VThree{PrivacyDeltaGt}).
The exact-copy and byte guards pass.
The outcome is \textbf{\VThree{Outcome}}.

\paragraph{Calibration of the decision.}
Under the comparison's sign-flip null, the statistical conditions alone pass in \VThree{StatPassPct} of \VThree{NullReplicates} replicates (Wilson interval \VThree{StatPassLowPct}--\VThree{StatPassHighPct}), far above the contract's \VThree{NullMaxRatePct}.
With \VThree{Families} families (\VThree{RegressionFamilies}, \VThree{BinaryFamilies} and \VThree{MulticlassFamilies} per task), the percentile interval is too narrow, and the headline $p$ should be read in that light.
With the guards a flip can move, the rate is \VThree{FlipRatePct} (upper Wilson bound \VThree{FlipHighPct}): the guards, not the interval, are what keep the rule calibrated on this panel.

\paragraph{Final look.}
After this report, Addendum~F of the pre-registration (SHA-256 \texttt{\VThree{FlPreregSha}}) reopened the release for one candidate build, \VThree{FlRcBuild}, which routes away from \texttt{DSE8} where the FIT-only privacy and fold guards fail (Appendix~\ref{app:arms}).
Before any score on data seeds \VThree{FlSeeds}, it froze the build, the schedule, a Holm family of $m=\VThree{FlHolmM}$ comparisons and a ship rule: the candidate ships if and only if it is PROMOTE against release~\VThree{BaseVersion} and its point difference against the released engine, \VThree{FlServedBuild} (this paper's \cli{auto-v8}), is at least zero.
On that look (\VThree{FlCells} informative cells, \VThree{FlFamilies} families), the candidate is \VThree{FlVsBaseOutcome} against release~\VThree{BaseVersion} at \VThree{FlVsBaseDelta} (Holm $p=\VThree{FlVsBasePHolm}$; all \VThree{FlVsBaseGuards} guards pass) but \VThree{FlVsServedOutcome} at \VThree{FlVsServedDelta} against \VThree{FlServedBuild} (\VThree{FlVsServedMatched} at $m=\VThree{FlMatchedMult}$).
The second condition fails, so the released engine remains \VThree{FlServedBuild}.
The pre-registered disclosures qualify the PROMOTE: \VThree{FlNonzeroFamilies} of \VThree{FlFamilies} families carry the gain, \VThree{FlTopSharePct} of it one multiclass family (\texttt{\VThree{FlTopFamily}}); the exact family sign-flip $p$ is \VThree{FlSignFlipP}; and the contract's sign-flip null promotes in \VThree{FlNullFlipRate} of replicates with or without the guards a flip can move, above its limit of \VThree{FlNullMaxRate}, so the rule is not calibrated on this look.
Descriptively, outside the Holm family, the released engine is \VThree{FlServedVsBaseOutcome} against release~\VThree{BaseVersion} at \VThree{FlServedVsBaseDelta}, failing the realism (\VThree{FlServedRealism}) and task lower-bound (\VThree{FlServedTaskLow}) guards.
The released engine's paired transfer interval spans zero on this look. It is the second fresh look, after data seeds \VThree{ReportSeeds} above, on which the released engine is HOLD against release~\VThree{BaseVersion}.
