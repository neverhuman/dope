\section{DOPE: a byte-bounded generative compiler}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Representation.}
A declared schema fixes the target, feature roles, original units and category semantics.
Fitting rows alone determine transforms and dictionaries. Numeric affine maps retain their
inverse; nominal levels are column-scoped indicators rather than ordered numbers. Grouped
driver tests recombine indicators and masks into their original semantic features. The
current native mixed profile is explicitly bounded; its capability is not evidence of
general mixed-table transfer.

\paragraph{Factorization.}
The symbolic program separates feature synthesis from conditional response:
\begin{equation}
  X\sim Q_{\theta}(X),\qquad
  Y=g_{\beta}(X)+\sigma_{\gamma}(X)\varepsilon,
  \quad \varepsilon\sim R_{\eta}.
  \label{eq:compiler}
\end{equation}
Quantized marginals and dependence operators represent $Q_{\theta}$; a bounded target
program represents $g_{\beta}$. The retained noise law is needed because a predictor alone
does not specify a joint distribution. Development residual profiles use out-of-fold
residuals to reduce the collapse caused by fitting noise to an interpolating predictor.
The default bounded-range feature sampler cannot establish unbounded tail fidelity.

\paragraph{Search and serialization.}
The native compiler tests dependence and target candidates, quantization precisions and
marginal resolutions. Candidate selection uses fitting-side heuristics, not the final
cross-model transfer measurement. An encoded candidate must fit its byte cap, including
the state required by its selected carrier. A proxy score is therefore an engineering
search objective, never a certificate that the downstream objective was preserved.
Native raw-unit, historical rank-space and SafeTensors carriers have separate charge
profiles; comparing their unadjusted sizes would change the estimand.

\paragraph{Why predictive moments matter.}
There is a precise special case behind the objective. Let $P$ and $Q$ denote real and
synthetic training distributions on the same frozen feature map. For ridge regression
with strictly positive regularization $\lambda$, write
$\Sigma_P=\mathbb{E}_P[xx^\top]$, $c_P=\mathbb{E}_P[xy]$, and
$w_P=\operatorname{inv}(\Sigma_P+\lambda I)c_P$, with analogous synthetic quantities. Then
\begin{equation}
 \lVert w_Q-w_P\rVert_2\leq
 \frac{\lVert c_Q-c_P\rVert_2+
       \lVert\Sigma_Q-\Sigma_P\rVert_2\lVert w_P\rVert_2}{\lambda}.
 \label{eq:ridge}
\end{equation}
Thus preserving the feature covariance and feature--target moments controls this
learner's parameter error even when a generic resemblance score is imperfect. This
standard perturbation argument (proof in the supplement) motivates moment diagnostics.
No corresponding result for trees, neural models, or arbitrary targets follows. Preserving only
$\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X]$ can still lose rare support, conditional variance and interactions.

\paragraph{Runtime boundary.}
The native product compiles and samples in Rust. The additive SafeTensors runtime uses
the same native random streams and marginal decoder, with optional batched Candle
inference for TVAE decoder heads. Historical activation behavior is preserved; an
explicit corrected activation profile matches the training formula. Accelerator execution,
cross-device parity and resulting transfer require separate hardware measurements.
