\section{Reversible table encoding and the quick-start ablation}
\label{app:encoding}
The compiler reads a numeric matrix on the unit interval with the target last; the quick start's
encoder (\path{examples/quickstart/dope_tables.py}) maps a raw table to it from the fit rows only and
inverts it exactly for categorical codes. A categorical column with few levels becomes one-hot
columns with the most frequent level as the dropped reference, because a full set of dummies sums
to one and is collinear with the intercept of the compiler's closed-form read-out. Any larger categorical column becomes one
target-ordered code, its levels placed by smoothed mean target with a small rank term that keeps
every code distinct. Skewed non-negative columns are log-transformed and every numeric column is
clipped at Tukey's far-out fences, never tighter than its outer percentiles, before rescaling.
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports every configuration of the ablation that chose these defaults,
on UCI Adult (fit rows subsampled per seed, official test file as query rows) and UCI Abalone
(a seeded split per seed). Transfer-in-the-loop selection scores the backends
\texttt{chow\_liu}, \texttt{compact\_neural\_residual\_symbolic}, \texttt{sparse\_gaussian\_copula}
and the built-in tournament by three-fold train-on-synthetic, test-on-held-out-fit-rows retention
with a linear and a gradient-boosted auditor, and compiles the winner on all fit rows. Evidence:
local engineering ablation on two public tables.

\section{Predictive-moment perturbation argument}
\label{app:moments}
Assume $P$ and $Q$ have finite second feature moments and finite feature--target moments,
and apply the same fixed feature map. All coefficients, including an intercept if present,
are regularized by $\lambda I$ with strictly positive $\lambda$. Define $w_P$ and $w_Q$ by the ridge
normal equations. Subtracting them gives
\[
 (\Sigma_Q+\lambda I)(w_Q-w_P)
 = (c_Q-c_P)-(\Sigma_Q-\Sigma_P)w_P.
\]
Since $\Sigma_Q$ is positive semidefinite,
the inverse operator has norm at most the reciprocal of $\lambda$.
Taking norms proves Equation~(\ref{app:momentbound}):
\begin{equation}
\lVert w_Q-w_P\rVert_2 \leq
\frac{\lVert c_Q-c_P\rVert_2+
\lVert\Sigma_Q-\Sigma_P\rVert_2\lVert w_P\rVert_2}{\lambda}.
\label{app:momentbound}
\end{equation}
For a fixed query distribution with second moment $\Sigma_*$, the mean squared
prediction discrepancy is
$(w_Q-w_P)^\top\Sigma_*(w_Q-w_P)$, at most
the product of $\lVert\Sigma_*\rVert_2$ and the squared Euclidean norm of $w_Q-w_P$.
This bounds prediction disagreement, not directly an arbitrary query excess risk;
that risk also contains the cross term with the real model's query residual.
Unregularized intercepts, changing encoders, feature scaling and finite-sample moment
estimation require their own analysis. No distribution-free multi-model result follows.

\section{Numerical deployment profiles}
The historical native activation uses twice the reciprocal square root of pi inside the tanh approximation;
the training implementation uses the conventional square root of two over pi. The difference is
preserved for old artifacts. The additive \texttt{tanh-v2} carrier uses the training
coefficient explicitly. An activation correction changes a sampler's distribution;
it must be evaluated as a new numerical profile and cannot relabel historical results.
CPU fixtures check nonzero decoder heads and original random streams. GPU parity and
transfer are unmeasured here. The initial SafeTensors carrier stores native DPK plus typed
TVAE tensors; the duplicate state and header are charged in full. Transport conformance
is not a compression improvement.
