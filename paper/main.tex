\begin{abstract}
Organizations cannot share their tables, but would often like outside learners to learn what those tables
teach. \emph{Transfer Description Length} (\MetricName) measures a synthetic-data method by the quantity
that matters for that use: the fewest bytes that carry a declared share of a table's predictive value, on
untouched real rows, to the worst of several learner families. D.O.P.E.\ compiles a table into a sampling
program of quantized marginals, a dependence tree, a compact network and a noise model, and charges every
byte its sampler reads. On a published panel of \EbPublishedTables{} tables the program keeps
\EbPublishedRobust{} of the real gain at \EbPublishedBytes{} bytes, TabSyn keeps \EbTabSynRobust{} with
\EbTabSynFactor{} times the bytes, and no generator's frontier lies above the program's. Against the real
rows themselves, given the best lossless codec, exact byte matching and step-matched training, the program
wins on \EbLargeWins{} of the \EbLargeLineages{} real-world tables at the study's cap of \cFitCap{} fit rows
(worst-learner gap \EbLargeGap{} [\EbLargeGapLo, \EbLargeGapHi]), an advantage that grows with the table's
size. Over all \EbLineages{} tables the gap is \EbGap{} [\EbGapLo, \EbGapHi], and the program's retention
plateaus: real rows reach the \cTau{} bar with fewer bytes on \EbTdlEightyData{} tables against
\EbTdlEightyDope{}. All results are selection-exposed development validation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A table is valuable because it changes what a learner can do. Most of the tables that would change the
most cannot leave the organizations that hold them, so the practical question is not whether a
synthetic table \emph{looks} like the real one but whether a learner trained on it \emph{learns} what the
real one teaches. Train-on-synthetic, test-on-real evaluation \citep{esteban2017rcgan,jordon2018measuring}
answers that question for one generator and one learner. It leaves out the size of the generator, which
ranges from kilobytes for copulas \citep{patki2016sdv} to hundreds of megabytes for diffusion and forest
models \citep{zhang2024tabsyn,jolicoeurmartineau2024forestdiffusion}.

We make the size the quantity being reduced. Copying a table needs bytes in proportion to its rows; a
description of what learners need from it needs bytes in proportion to its structure. A program that is
small, charged byte for byte and judged only by what learners recover from its samples therefore measures
how much predictive structure was separated from noise \citep{rissanen1978modeling}, is cheap to audit and
ship, and has little room to memorize rows.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{A transfer-per-byte KPI.} \MetricName{} and its frontier, the share of tables that clear
  a worst-learner transfer bar at each budget, with hard gates against empty, padded or externally
  decoded artifacts (\cref{sec:metric}).
  \item \textbf{Byte-bounded sampling programs.} The DOPE program and compiler, with three changes that
  make bytes honest and the method reproducible: values stored as quantization-grid codes, the headline
  network trained natively on a CPU, and an invertible plan that compiles raw mixed-type tables and
  decodes samples back to the source schema (\cref{sec:method}).
  \item \textbf{Why it works.} Retention under squared loss is the share of signal variance the program's
  conditional mean explains, and under log loss the share of label information it delivers; a two-part
  code argument says when a program beats the data at equal bytes (\cref{sec:theory}).
  \item \textbf{Evidence.} A worst-learner re-reading of a published generator panel, a new equal-byte
  study against real rows on \EbLineages{} real-world tables and all \EbPanelLineages{} panel lineages, a
  scaling study up to \EbScaleMaxN{} rows,
  a raw mixed-type table (UCI Adult), and ablations including a negative result for an exact Gaussian
  copula (\cref{sec:experiments}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Transfer Description Length}
\label{sec:metric}

For a table $d$ split into fit rows and untouched test rows, a learner $m$ and an artifact $A$, let
$\Lnull$, $\Lreal$ and $\Lsyn(A)$ be the test losses of $m$ when it predicts the training mean (or prior),
when it is trained on the fit rows, and when it is trained only on samples drawn from $A$. Transfer is
\begin{equation}
  T_{m}(A) = \frac{\Lnull - \Lsyn(A)}{\Lnull - \Lreal},
  \qquad \Tr(A) = \min_{f} T_f(A),
  \label{eq:transfer}
\end{equation}
where $T_f$ averages the learners of family $f$ and the outer $\min$ runs over the required families (boosted
trees, neural networks, linear models). A table is informative for a learner only when its real gain
clears a declared floor; uninformative cells are excluded, never scored. For the frontier, $T$ is clipped
to the unit interval, so a learner that gains more from synthetic than from real rows cannot offset
another.

\MetricName{} at threshold $\tau$ is the smallest budget $B$ on a fixed grid for which some artifact of
at most $B$ charged bytes reaches $\Tr \geq \tau$ (with its lower confidence bound) and passes the hard
gates: copy and near-copy checks, a cleartext scan, positive lift over predictor-only and
independent-marginal controls, and a byte ledger that reconciles with a clean-room sampler. Charged
bytes include every byte the sampler reads: schema, transforms, dictionaries, parameters and framing; a
shared decoder or checkpoint is charged as sampling closure. The headline operating point is
\HeadlinePoint{} ($\tau_T = \TauT$, $\tau_D = \TauD$), where $D$ is a driver-agreement gate. Over a
panel, the frontier $C(B)$ is the share of tables that qualify at budget $B$; failed and missing tables
stay in the denominator. An empty generator scores zero coverage at every budget, padding only moves an
artifact to a larger budget, and a hidden decoder is charged, so none of them can game the frontier.
\Cref{fig:frontier} plots $C(B)$ at $\tau = \cTau$ with the driver gate unmeasured, which we label as
a transfer-only frontier.

\section{Programs and the compiler}
\label{sec:method}

A DOPE program factorizes the joint law as
$Q(x,y) = c_\theta\bigl(F_{\One}(x_{\One}),\dots,F_p(x_p)\bigr)\prod_j f_j(x_j)\, q(y \mid x)$ with
\begin{equation}
  m(x) = c + \beta^\top x_S + v^\top \tanh\!\left(W x_S + b\right),
  \qquad y = m(x) + \sigma(x)\,\varepsilon,
  \label{eq:program}
\end{equation}
where $S$ holds the $\cScreen$ inputs with the largest absolute rank correlation with $y$, $W$ has
$\cHidden$ hidden units, and $\log\sigma(x)$ is linear in $\cNoiseInputs$ screened inputs and clamped
between residual deciles; binary targets use a calibrated Bernoulli head instead. The marginals $F_j$
are quantile splines with $\cKnots$ knots and a missing rate, and $c_\theta$ is a Gaussian copula
restricted to a tree in which each column has one parent among the previous $\cParents$. The network
holds $\cParams$ numbers whatever the number of rows; it is where the bytes go. Every parameter is
quantized on a grid fixed by the header bit width: unit values to $\Two^{b}-\One$ levels and unbounded
values to steps of $\Two^{-(b-\Two)}$. \Cref{alg:dope} lists compile and sample.

\begin{figure}[t]
\small
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-\Two\fboxsep-\Two\fboxrule\relax}

\textbf{Algorithm~\thealgo.} Compile a table plan and program; sample from the program alone.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Plan} (raw tables): numbers to the unit interval, log-scaling columns with skewness above
$\cSkew$; categories to one code per column, levels ordered by smoothed target mean; dates to day counts;
missing cells kept.
\item \textbf{Compile} (fit rows only): fit marginals and missing rates; fit the dependence tree on
rank correlations; screen $S$; train \eqref{eq:program} with AdamW (rate $\cRate$, weight decay $\cDecay$,
element-wise clipping at $\cClip$) for $\cSteps$ full-batch steps; refit $(c,\beta,v)$ in closed form;
fit the noise model on the residuals; quantize; encode with packed values.
\item \textbf{Sample}$(A, N)$: draw $z$ from the tree copula; $x_j \leftarrow F_j^{-\One}(\Phi(z_j))$;
$y \leftarrow m(x) + \sigma(x)\varepsilon$; mask features at their missing rates; decode through the plan.
\end{itemize}
\end{minipage}}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Three changes in this release.} \emph{Packed values.} The historical codec wrote every unit
value as a sixteen-bit word and every unbounded value as a scaled varint, so the header bit width changed
precision but hardly bytes. A header flag now stores grid codes (one byte at $q\cBits$) and refuses a
kernel whose parameters are off its grid; old artifacts are untouched. For the headline shape the decoded
program, and so every sample, is bit-identical to the historical encoding; for shapes with copula edges or
bit widths whose grids differ from the historical sixteen-bit grid, parameters agree only to the codec's
tolerance, so samples can differ slightly. \emph{Native training.} Without libtorch, earlier CPU builds substituted fixed random
hidden weights. The network now trains in Rust with the published optimizer settings
\citep{loshchilov2019decoupled}, deterministically for a seed. \emph{Table plans.} A raw CSV compiles into
one artifact holding a binary column plan and the program; samples decode to the source header, category
labels, integer columns, dates and missing markers. Ordered category codes carry each level's main effect
in one coefficient; one-hot dummies would cost bytes per level and let independently sampled dummies
violate the one-of-$k$ constraint, so dummies are left to the downstream learner's view.

\section{Why samples from a program transfer}
\label{sec:theory}

Write $m_P(x) = \E_P[y \mid x]$ and $s_P = \E_P[\Var_P(y\mid x)]$ (a variance), and define $m_Q$ for the
program. Consider a learner that is consistent on the support of the law it is trained on, in the limit of
many synthetic rows and many real rows, so that both estimation errors vanish; \cref{prop:scaling} keeps
the real sample finite.

\begin{proposition}[squared loss]
\label{prop:squared}
If the support of $Q_X$ covers that of $P_X$, then
$T = \One - \E_P\Sq{\bigl[m_Q(X) - m_P(X)\bigr]} \big/ \Var_P\bigl(m_P(X)\bigr)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For any predictor $g$, $\E_P\Sq{(y - g(X))} = s_P + \E_P\Sq{(m_P - g)}$. The null predicts $\E_P y$ and
loses $s_P + \Var(m_P)$; the real learner converges to $m_P$ and the synthetic learner to $m_Q$ on the
support of $P_X$. Substitute into \eqref{eq:transfer}.
\end{proof}

Retention is the share of the true signal variance that the program's conditional mean explains,
measured under the real covariates. The joint feature model and the noise model enter only through
support and finite-sample variance, which is why the compiler spends its bytes on $m$.

\begin{corollary}[linear and flexible learners]
\label{cor:support}
A linear learner trained on $Q$ converges to $\Sigma_Q^{-\One}\operatorname{Cov}_Q(X, m_Q)$, so it
transfers fully whenever $Q_X$ matches the first two moments of $P_X$ and $m_Q$ matches the linear
projection of $m_P$. For a flexible learner with error $e = f_Q - m_Q$ on $Q$, and $w = dP_X/dQ_X$,
$\E_P\Sq{e} \le \bigl(\E_Q\Sq{w}\bigr)^{\One/\Two}\bigl(\E_Q e^{\Four}\bigr)^{\One/\Two}$
with $\E_Q\Sq{w} = \One + \chi^{\Two}(P_X\,\|\,Q_X)$ \citep{shimodaira2000improving}.
\end{corollary}

The corollary explains a control in the published panel: a ridge regressor sampled with independent
marginals keeps nearly all of a linear learner's gain but far less of CatBoost's, because trees learn
$m_Q$ only where $Q_X$ puts mass. It also says what the compiler should measure and currently does not:
the divergence between real and synthetic covariates.

\begin{proposition}[log loss]
\label{prop:logloss}
Under the same support condition, for a binary target, $T = \One - \E_P\,\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\mathrm{Ber}(p_P(X))\,\|\,\mathrm{Ber}(p_Q(X))\bigr)\big/ I_P(X;Y)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\E_P[-\log p_Q(Y\mid X)] = H(Y\mid X) + \E_P\mathrm{KL}(p_P\|p_Q)$, and
$\Lnull - \Lreal = H(Y) - H(Y\mid X) = I(X;Y)$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{When a program beats the data.} A two-part code describes a table as a model plus residuals
given the model \citep{rissanen1978modeling}. Real rows ship both; a program ships the model and replaces
the residuals with a few bytes of noise model that regenerate fresh residuals.

\begin{proposition}[equal-byte scaling]
\label{prop:scaling}
Suppose a learner trained on $k$ real rows has excess risk $C/k$ with $C = d_{\mathrm{eff}}\, s_P$ and the
real reference uses $n$ rows. The rows needed to match a program with retention $T$ are
$k(T) = C\big/\bigl((\One - T)\Var(m_P) + T C/n\bigr)$, which tends to
$\frac{s_P}{\Var(m_P)}\,\frac{d_{\mathrm{eff}}}{\One - T}$ as $n$ grows. At $b$ bits per value the
data-equivalence ratio is $k(T)\,(p+\One)\,b \,/\, (\Eight\,|A|)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Solve $(\Lnull - L_k)/(\Lnull - L_n) = T$ with $L_k = s_P + C/k$.
\end{proof}

A program's size is fixed by its shape, not by the rows, so the proposition predicts that the
data-equivalence ratio grows with the rows until the whole table caps it, that it grows with the noise share
over $\One - T$, that tiny tables are cheapest to ship whole, and that at strict thresholds the program's
approximation error becomes the bottleneck. \Cref{sec:scaling} tests these predictions. \MetricName{} is thus an operational rate--distortion function
with distortion $\One - \Tr$; the real rows define a second curve, the distortion of the trivial code.
We make no claim that either estimates a Shannon rate--distortion function.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Protocol.} Learners are the historical evaluator's: ordinary least squares, a one-hidden-layer
MLP and CatBoost \citep{prokhorenkova2018catboost}, fixed seeds. Retention uses mean squared error on the
target scaled by fit-row statistics (log loss for binary targets) and synthetic size $\cSynthetic$.
\emph{Generator panel}: the frozen ledger of the published validation run on PMLB regression lineages
\citep{olson2017pmlb,romano2021pmlb}, re-read on the worst of three learners with the charged bytes the
ledger records. \emph{Equal-byte study}: the \EbLineages{} real-world lineages of that panel after removing
content duplicates, shuffled with a fixed seed; a disjoint test set of \cTestShare{} of the rows, capped at
\cFitCap{}, and up to \cFitCap{} of the remaining rows as fit rows. DOPE programs are a five-rung ladder of
packed programs with three fit seeds each. The real-row arm ships the largest uniform subset of fit rows
whose eight- or sixteen-bit codes, sorted and stored column-major, fit the budget under the best of LZMA2,
bzip2 and deflate, with three subset seeds, at every grid budget and at each rung's own median size, so
equal bytes are exact. The real-row arm's codec, byte matching and MLP protocol favor the real rows: the MLP
keeps its better test loss of training on the subset as given or resampled to the synthetic size, early
stopped on held-out distinct rows, since its epoch budget would otherwise starve small subsets of optimizer
steps; the real reference's MLP gets the same treatment, and the whole table scores as the reference
itself. The DOPE side is not neutral either: its profile was selected on this panel and its frontier takes
the best rung. Both arms pay the same two single-precision floats per
column for the fit-row scaling. Seeds aggregate by the median; the bytes real rows need to match a level
interpolate the real-row frontier in log bytes. Intervals are percentile bootstraps over tables. Every number is development validation and
selection-exposed: the published profile was chosen on validation rows of the same panel.

\Cref{fig:frontier}(b) and \cref{fig:paired} compare a program with the data it was compiled from. Whether
a program beats its data depends on how much larger the table is than the program. On the
\EbLargeLineages{} tables with \cFitCap{} fit rows, the program keeps \EbLargeGap{} [\EbLargeGapLo,
\EbLargeGapHi] more of the real gain than the most real rows that fit in exactly its bytes, for the worst
learner (\EbLargeWins{} wins, \EbLargeLosses{} losses), and real rows need a median \EbRatioLarge{} times
its bytes to match it. Learner by learner the median differences are \EbLargeLinearGap{} for least squares
(\EbLargeLinearWins{} wins, \EbLargeLinearLosses{} losses), \EbLargeMlpGap{} for the MLP and
\EbLargeCatboostGap{} for CatBoost (\EbLargeCatboostWins{} wins, \EbLargeCatboostLosses{} losses): the
worst-learner contrast is larger because on a few dozen real rows some learner often keeps nothing. Across
all \EbLineages{} tables the gap rises with the whole table's size over the program's (Spearman
\EbRhoSize{}; \EbRhoSizeOutgrown{} on the \EbRhoSizeOutgrownN{} tables whose whole table is larger than the
program, since a table that fits in the program's bytes cannot lose to it) and crosses zero where the
compressed table is about as large as a program: tables of a few hundred rows are cheapest shipped whole,
and on the two apnea tables the program itself fails. Over all tables the median gap is \EbGap{} [\EbGapLo,
\EbGapHi] (\EbWins{} wins, \EbLosses{} losses).

Two sensitivities bound the protocol's influence. Training the real rows as given, without matching the
MLP's optimizer steps, raises the large-table gap to \EbAsGivenLargeGap{}; leaving the MLP out of the
worst-learner score lowers it to \EbNoMlpLargeGap{} [\EbNoMlpLargeGapLo, \EbNoMlpLargeGapHi]
(\EbNoMlpLargeWins{} wins, \EbNoMlpLargeLosses{} losses). Part of the advantage is therefore that a flexible
learner generalizes better from many samples of a smooth conditional mean than from a few dozen real rows.

The frontier shows the other regime. The program's retention plateaus below the bar on hard tables, while
enough real rows clear it: on the large tables real rows overtake the program at a few kilobytes, and to
reach $\Tr \ge \cTau$ they need fewer bytes on \EbTdlEightyData{} of \EbLineages{} tables against
\EbTdlEightyDope{} for the program (\EbTdlNinetyData{} against \EbTdlNinetyDope{} at \cTauStrict). A
$\cParams$-number conditional mean is a lossy channel; raising its ceiling at fixed bytes is the main open
problem. On all \EbPanelLineages{} lineages of the panel, simulated families included, the large-table gap
is \EbPanelLargeGap{} [\EbPanelLargeGapLo, \EbPanelLargeGapHi] (\EbPanelLargeWins{} wins,
\EbPanelLargeLosses{} losses; supplement, \emph{Full panel and parity}).

\Cref{fig:scaling} shows the design property behind the comparison: a program's size is set by its shape,
so it does not grow with the rows, while the whole table does. The equal-byte gap rising with the table's
size over the program's (\cref{fig:paired}) is the clearest confirmation of \cref{prop:scaling}. Across tables the data-equivalence ratio
rises with the fit rows (Spearman \EbRhoRows{} over \EbRhoRowsN{} tables with a finite ratio, and
\EbPanelRhoRows{} over \EbPanelRhoRowsN{} panel tables), but part of that rise is mechanical: the ratio is
bounded by the whole table's size, which grows with the rows. The proposition's proxy, the noise share over
$\One - T$ times the row cost, ranks the ratio on the \EbRhoProxyN{} real tables with \cFitCap{} fit rows
(Spearman \EbRhoProxy{}) and, more weakly, on the panel's \EbPanelRhoProxyN{} large tables
(\EbPanelRhoProxy{}). About half of those are noise-free physics equations, where real-row learning curves are
limited by the learners' approximation error rather than by the noise-driven excess risk $C/k$ the
proposition assumes. The proxy ranks tables but does not predict the ratio's level; a proper test of
\cref{prop:scaling} needs per-table learning curves.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Tabular generators} fit copulas and dependence trees \citep{patki2016sdv,chow1968dependence},
sequential models \citep{nowok2016synthpop}, adversarial and variational networks \citep{xu2019ctgan},
forests \citep{watson2023arf,jolicoeurmartineau2024forestdiffusion} and diffusion models
\citep{kotelnikov2023tabddpm,zhang2024tabsyn}; marginal-based synthesizers add formal privacy
\citep{zhang2014privbayes,mckenna2022aim}. They optimize a joint likelihood or a denoising objective and
ship their weights; DOPE optimizes the conditional mean that learners need under a byte charge.
\textbf{Compact models of tables} include probabilistic programs \citep{saad2019bayesian} and CrossCat
\citep{mansinghka2016crosscat}, which target the joint distribution and inference rather than transfer per
byte. \textbf{Distillation} keeps learned behavior in a few examples \citep{wang2018distillation} or a
smaller model \citep{hinton2015distilling}; a program instead draws fresh rows, and its predictor-only
control isolates what sampling adds. \textbf{Evaluation} of synthetic data spans utility and fidelity
measures \citep{alaa2022faithful,qian2023synthcity} and privacy attacks \citep{stadler2022groundhog}.
Description-length and bottleneck ideas \citep{rissanen1978modeling,tishby2000bottleneck} motivate the
framing; \MetricName{} is an operational measure, not a code-length estimate.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

All results are development validation and selection-exposed; the sealed test has not been opened. The
study's threshold budgets are point estimates: the driver gate is unmeasured, and neither lower confidence
bounds nor the copy, lift and ledger gates were applied, so \cref{fig:frontier} is a transfer-only frontier.
Packed artifacts are not yet accepted by the repository's qualification bundle, which predates the flag.
The real-row arm picks its MLP training protocol on the test rows; that selection favors the real rows. The
generator comparison re-reads a historical panel whose splits cannot be rebuilt, so it is not paired with
the equal-byte study. The real-world panel is regression on PMLB tables with at most \cFitCap{} fit rows;
classification evidence is one table. A program is conditioned on one target. Copy, membership and
attribute checks are empirical diagnostics, not formal differential privacy and not HIPAA
de-identification. At strict transfer thresholds enough real rows reach the bar with fewer bytes than the
program on most tables; raising the program's ceiling at fixed bytes, for example by distilling the
conditional mean from a strong teacher, is the main open problem.
