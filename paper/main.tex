\begin{abstract}
\noindent Tabular data is often impossible to share, and a multi-megabyte generator trained on it is opaque and
costly to audit. We ask how few bytes a sampling program needs so that learners trained only on its samples
recover what learners trained on the real rows learn. We define \MetricLong{} (\MetricName), an operational
rate--distortion measure that charges every byte a sampler reads and makes transfer across learner families a
constraint, not a weighted term. At the population limit, retention is one minus the conditional-mean error
under the real covariate law (squared loss) and the delivered share of feature--label mutual information (log
loss). This motivates \dope{}, a compiler of target-explicit programs: quantized marginals, a compact
dependence model, an explicit conditional mean and a noise model. On a selection-exposed validation panel of
\CFLineages{} PMLB regression tables, \dope{}'s median program holds \CFDopeBytes{} bytes of dataset-specific
state, \CFByteRatio{}$\times$ fewer than TabSyn, and its worst-auditor retention across CatBoost, linear and
MLP auditors cannot be separated from TabSyn's (paired difference \CFPairHL{} [\CFPairLo{}, \CFPairHi{}]).
It ranks first of \CFGenerators{} generators in retained lift per kibibyte, \CFLiftRatio{}$\times$ the
runner-up. The official test split is reserved for one pre-registered run.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A dataset is valuable because it changes what a learner can do. Security, legal and competitive constraints
keep most tabular data inside the organization that collected it, so external methods and pretrained
foundation models are rarely evaluated on the data that matters. Synthetic data is the usual answer, but
two practices make it hard to trust. Generators are judged by how much their samples resemble the source,
or by one downstream learner, while the size of the generator goes uncounted; and released generators are
large: the neural and diffusion generators we compare against ship from hundreds of kilobytes to hundreds
of megabytes of weights \citep{xu2019ctgan,zhang2024tabsyn,jolicoeurmartineau2024forestdiffusion}.

We take the downstream objective as the definition of quality and the byte as the unit of cost. A compiler
reads the real fit rows and emits a program; a sampler reads only that program; learners from several
families are trained on its samples and scored on real rows the compiler never saw. The question is how
small the program can be while the learners still recover what the real rows teach them. Copying rows does
not answer it: the bytes grow with the table and copy gates fail. Memorizing a table in a checkpoint does not
answer it either, because every byte the sampler opens is charged.

\paragraph{Insight.} Train-on-synthetic, test-on-real evaluation \citep{esteban2017rcgan,jordon2018measuring}
depends on far less than the joint distribution. Proposition~\ref{prop:retention} shows that, at the
population limit, retention is determined by how closely the synthetic regression function tracks the real
one on the real covariate distribution, plus enough covariate support for a flexible learner to find it
(Corollary~\ref{cor:support}). A generator that spends bytes on anything else buys no transfer. \dope{}
therefore stores the conditional mean explicitly and keeps the feature model compact.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textbf{A metric with units.} \MetricName{} is the smallest tested dataset-specific payload at which
  transfer clears a lower-confidence threshold in every required learner family, drivers agree, and copy,
  cleartext and byte-accounting gates pass (Section~\ref{sec:metric}).
  \item \textbf{A short theory of transfer.} Retention identities for squared and log loss, a corollary that
  predicts which auditors need a dependence model, and a size bound that does not grow with the table
  (Section~\ref{sec:theory}).
  \item \textbf{A compiler.} \dope{} emits typed, quantized, inspectable programs; a reversible table
  encoder and a transfer-in-the-loop backend selector bring real mixed-type tables to it
  (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
  \item \textbf{Evidence with receipts.} On the validation panel \dope{} is the smallest program on the
  transfer frontier and first in retained lift per kibibyte; the predictor-only control matches the
  pattern the theory predicts; every number here is rendered from hash-pinned ledgers
  (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Problem and metric}
\label{sec:metric}

A table is split into fit rows $D_{\mathrm{fit}}$ and untouched query rows. A compiler maps $D_{\mathrm{fit}}$,
a declared target and a byte budget $B$ to an artifact $A$ with charged size $|A| \le B$; a sampler reads
only $A$. For an auditor $m$, a learning recipe from family $f$, let $\Lnull$, $\Lreal$ and $\Lsyn(A)$ be its
mean losses on the query rows when it learns nothing from the table, when it is trained on $D_{\mathrm{fit}}$,
and when it is trained on samples from $A$. Retention is
\begin{equation}
  T_m(A) = \frac{\Lnull - \Lsyn(A)}{\Lnull - \Lreal}.
  \label{eq:transfer}
\end{equation}
A cell counts only when the real gain clears a declared floor; receipts keep $T$ unclipped. Within a family
retention is averaged and across required families the worst family is kept, $\Trobust(A) = \min_f T_f(A)$,
so adding a family can never raise it. Driver fidelity $\Drobust$ compares grouped permutation importances
of real- and synthetic-trained instances of the same recipe on the same real rows, normalized by the
agreement two real-trained instances reach. An artifact qualifies when
\begin{equation}
  \LCB\bigl(\Trobust(A)\bigr) \ge \tau_T, \qquad \Drobust(A) \ge \tau_D, \qquad G(A) = \text{pass},
  \label{eq:qualify}
\end{equation}
where $G$ is the conjunction of hard gates. \MetricName{} is the charged payload of the qualifying artifact at
the smallest tested budget, reported with its grid interval, at the operating point \HeadlinePoint{}
($\tau_T = $ \TauT, $\tau_D = $ \TauD) of the \ContractState{} contract \ContractId{} over the grid \ByteGrid.
The charge counts every byte whose value depends on the data and that sampling reads (schema, transforms,
dictionaries, framing and sidecars); the runtime closure is reported beside it.

\section{Why a target-explicit program transfers}
\label{sec:theory}

Let $P$ be the real distribution with regression function $m_P(x) = \E_P[y \mid x]$ and covariate law $P_X$.
A program induces a synthetic distribution $Q$ with regression function $m_Q$ and covariate law $Q_X$. Write
$f_Q$ for the limit of an auditor trained on unboundedly many synthetic rows, $\MSE_{P_X}(f, g) =
\E_{P_X}[(f(X) - g(X))(f(X) - g(X))]$, and $\Delta(A) = (\Lsyn(A) - \Lreal)/(\Lnull - \Lreal)$ for the
retention deficit, so that retention is one minus $\Delta$.

\begin{proposition}[Retention under squared loss]
\label{prop:retention}
If the null predicts $\E_P[y]$ and the real-trained auditor reaches the Bayes risk, then
\[
  \Delta(A) = \frac{\MSE_{P_X}\!\bigl(f_Q, m_P\bigr)}{\Var_P\bigl(m_P(X)\bigr)} .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because $\E_P[y - m_P(X) \mid X]$ vanishes, $\E_P[(y - f(X))(y - f(X))] = \E_P[(y - m_P(X))(y - m_P(X))] +
\MSE_{P_X}(f, m_P)$ for every $f$. Applying this to $f_Q$, to $m_P$ and to the constant $\E_P[y]$ gives
$\Lsyn - \Lreal = \MSE_{P_X}(f_Q, m_P)$ and $\Lnull - \Lreal = \Var_P(m_P(X))$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Retention under log loss]
\label{prop:logloss}
For a binary target with $p(x) = P(y \text{ positive} \mid x)$, the base-rate null and a Bayes real-trained
auditor,
\[
  \Delta(A) = \frac{\E_{P_X}\bigl[\KL\bigl(\Ber(p(X)) \,\big\|\, \Ber(f_Q(X))\bigr)\bigr]}{I(X; Y)} .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The excess log loss of a predictor over $p$ is the expected Kullback--Leibler divergence, and the excess of
the base rate over $p$ is $H(Y) - H(Y \mid X) = I(X; Y)$.
\end{proof}

At the population limit, then, retention under log loss is the fraction of the feature--label mutual
information that the program delivers to a learner. Finite-sample retention is not a mutual-information
estimator, since its denominator is the auditor's own real gain, and \MetricName{} makes no such claim; the
identity says what the quantity converges to and why it is the right object to compress. Two consequences
distinguish auditor families.

\begin{corollary}[Linear auditors see only the projection]
\label{cor:linear}
If $m_Q$ is linear in the features and $Q_X$ has a non-singular second-moment matrix, least squares on
synthetic rows converges to the coefficients of $m_Q$ whatever the dependence in $Q_X$.
\end{corollary}

A predictor-only sampler, a fitted ridge regression over independently drawn features, should therefore keep
almost all of the linear auditor's retention and lose most of what a flexible auditor gains from
nonlinearity. Section~\ref{sec:controls} tests this prediction.

\begin{corollary}[Flexible auditors need support]
\label{cor:support}
For a local averaging auditor trained on $n_{\mathrm{syn}}$ synthetic rows, the variance of its estimate of
$m_Q$, weighted by the real covariate law, scales with $\E_{Q_X}[w\,w] / n_{\mathrm{syn}}$, where
$w = dP_X / dQ_X$: the second moment of the importance weight, which sets the effective sample size of
importance weighting under covariate shift \citep{shimodaira2000covariate} and grows with the Pearson
divergence of $Q_X$ from $P_X$.
\end{corollary}

The dependence model pays for itself by keeping $Q_X$ on the support of $P_X$, and more synthetic rows help.
Independent marginals put mass where the real table has none, which is why they transfer almost nothing even
with exact marginals.

\begin{proposition}[The program does not grow with the table]
\label{prop:size}
Let $p(A)$ count the quantized parameters of a \dope{} program: the knots of every marginal, the dependence
parameters, the screened inputs and hidden units of the target model, and the noise parameters. With $b$ bits
per parameter, $|A| \le c_{\mathrm{frame}} + b\,p(A)$, and $p(A)$ depends on the feature count and the
compiler's ladders but not on the row count $n$.
\end{proposition}

Bits per original cell therefore fall in proportion to the reciprocal of $n$; Section~\ref{sec:demo} shows the
effect on a public table.

\begin{remark}[\MetricName{} as an operational rate--distortion function]
Define $R(\tau) = \inf\{|A| : T(A) \ge \tau\}$ and let $\bar\tau$, one minus $\tau$, be the tolerated
deficit. By Proposition~\ref{prop:retention}, $T(A) \ge \tau$ asks the induced regression function to lie
within $\bar\tau \Var_P(m_P(X))$ of $m_P$ in mean square under $P_X$, so $R$ is the description cost of a regression function at a prescribed distortion, with the
constraint that the code be a sampler. \MetricName{} estimates $R$ on a byte grid with the gates of
Section~\ref{sec:metric}; the frontier is the whole curve. Two-part code length \citep{rissanen1978modeling}
and the information bottleneck \citep{tishby2000bottleneck} motivate the framing but are not claimed as the
measure.
\end{remark}

\section{\dope{}: compiling a table into a program}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{The program.} A \dope{} artifact is a small typed program with four parts. \emph{Marginals:} for
every feature, a missing rate and either an exact grid over the observed values or a monotone quantile spline.
\emph{Dependence:} a latent Gaussian vector with structured correlation, chosen from independence, a
Chow--Liu tree \citep{chow1968dependence}, a triangular autoregression, a sparse Gaussian copula
\citep{patki2016sdv}, a factor model, a truncated vine \citep{aas2009paircopula} and a two-component mixture;
features are the inverse marginal transforms of the latent coordinates. \emph{Target:} an explicit
conditional mean from a small family, the most expressive being the compact neural residual
\[
  \hat y(x) = a + \beta^{\top} x_S + v^{\top} \tanh(W x_S + b),
\]
on a support $S$ of at most twelve features screened by absolute rank correlation with the target.
\emph{Noise:} homoscedastic, log-linear heteroscedastic, or a calibration for binary targets. Every parameter
is quantized from a bit ladder, and \texttt{dope-kernel inspect} prints the program as an S-expression.

\paragraph{Compile and sample.} The compiler fits each candidate of a bounded tournament (dependence family,
target family, knot and bit ladders) on the fit rows, encodes it, and charges its bytes; sampling draws the
latent vector, maps it through the marginals, evaluates the target model and adds noise, reading nothing but
the program. The validation panel used a fixed profile; Section~\ref{sec:demo} replaces the tournament's
train-only proxies with \emph{transfer-in-the-loop selection}, which scores a small portfolio of backends by
cross-validated train-on-synthetic, test-on-held-out-fit-rows retention, using fit rows only.

\paragraph{Real tables.} The compiler reads a numeric matrix on the unit interval with the target last. A
reversible encoder fitted on the fit rows maps a raw table to it: each categorical column becomes a single
target-ordered code (levels placed by their smoothed mean target, so the code carries the main effect
linearly and decodes exactly), with one-hot columns and a dropped reference level as an option; skewed
non-negative columns are log-transformed, every numeric column is clipped at Tukey's far-out fences
and rescaled; dates become numbers and missing cells stay missing. The encoder's state is a compact binary
blob charged next to the program, and its inverse returns synthetic rows with the original names, labels and
types.

\section{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

The validation panel holds rights-cleared PMLB regression tables \citep{olson2017pmlb,romano2021pmlb}; the
analyses below use the \CFLineages{} tables on which all three task-trained auditors are informative, so every
method shares one denominator. Auditors are CatBoost \citep{prokhorenkova2018catboost}, ordinary least squares
and a one-hidden-layer MLP; synthetic size is $4n$ for $n$ fit rows. Comparators are TabSyn with a scaled
schedule \citep{zhang2024tabsyn}, author-default ARF \citep{watson2023arf}, TVAE and CTGAN
\citep{xu2019ctgan}, a historical Forest-Flow block \citep{jolicoeurmartineau2024forestdiffusion}, and density
baselines; controls are a predictor-only sampler and real rows resampled to $4n$, a row-count diagnostic that
is not a generator. The worst-auditor value of a fit is the smallest of the three auditors' sample-seed
medians, a lineage value is the median of its fits, and intervals come from a nested bootstrap over
simulated-family clusters, lineages and fit seeds; paired comparisons report the Hodges--Lehmann estimate.
Bytes are median charged artifact bytes, including the projection sidecar. The panel also informed
\dope{}'s profile choice, so every number is a selection-exposed validation estimate; the official test split
is sealed.

\subsection{The controls confirm the theory}
\label{sec:controls}

Corollary~\ref{cor:linear} predicts that a predictor-only sampler keeps the linear auditor's retention and
loses the flexible auditors'. It does: its median retention is \CFPredLinear{} under the linear auditor,
\CFPredMlp{} under the MLP and \CFPredCatBoost{} under CatBoost. \dope{}'s nonlinear residual and dependence
model close most of that gap, reaching \CFDopeCatBoost{} under CatBoost, \CFDopeLinear{} under the linear
auditor and \CFDopeMlp{} under the MLP on the same lineages. Retention above one under the MLP is a
finite-sample effect that Proposition~\ref{prop:retention} excludes only at the population limit: labels drawn
from a smooth conditional mean are easier to learn than noisy real labels, and resampled real rows at $4n$
reach \CFBootMlp{}, so row count explains part of it. Receipts keep such values unclipped and never let them
offset a failure.

\subsection{A public mixed-type table}
\label{sec:demo}

The quick start runs the whole pipeline on the UCI Adult table: \QSFitRows{} fit rows with numeric and
categorical columns, a binary target, and the \QSHoldoutRows{} rows of the official test file as untouched
query rows. Transfer-in-the-loop selection chose \texttt{\QSBackend}; the program and the encoder state take
\QSStateBytes{} bytes, \QSCsvRatio{}$\times$ smaller than the fit table as CSV and \QSGzipRatio{}$\times$ smaller
than its \texttt{gzip} archive, and retention under log loss is \QSRetCatBoost{} for CatBoost, \QSRetLinear{}
for logistic regression and \QSRetMlp{} for the MLP. A five-seed ablation on Adult and UCI Abalone, reported in full in the
supplement, chose the defaults; \QSAblationSummary{} No fixed backend is best on both tables, which is the
case for selecting by transfer. This is a local engineering demonstration on two public tables, not a
benchmark result.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Tabular generators range from copulas, Bayesian networks and sequential trees
\citep{chow1968dependence,patki2016sdv,nowok2016synthpop,zhang2014privbayes} to adversarial and variational
models \citep{xu2019ctgan,park2018tablegan,zhao2021ctabgan}, diffusion and score models
\citep{kotelnikov2023tabddpm,zhang2024tabsyn,kim2023stasy,shi2025tabdiff}, forests
\citep{watson2023arf,jolicoeurmartineau2024forestdiffusion}, language models
\citep{borisov2023great,solatorio2023realtabformer} and prior-fitted networks \citep{ma2023tabpfgen}; marginal
synthesizers with formal privacy \citep{mckenna2021mst,mckenna2022aim} also fit small explicit models.
Probabilistic-program synthesis \citep{saad2019bayesian} and CrossCat \citep{mansinghka2016crosscat} produce
inspectable models of the joint table; \dope{} instead targets a declared downstream objective and charges
every byte. Dataset and model distillation \citep{wang2018distillation,hinton2015distilling} compress data or
models for one learner; generative compression names learned media codecs \citep{santurkar2017generative}.
Utility, fidelity and privacy evaluation of synthetic data
\citep{snoke2018utility,alaa2022faithful,qian2023synthcity,stadler2022groundhog,platzer2021holdout} and
tabular benchmarks \citep{mcelfresh2023tabzilla,erickson2026tabarena} broaden train-on-synthetic, test-on-real
evaluation; our additions are the byte axis, worst-family transfer and the controls.

\section{Limitations and broader impact}
\label{sec:limits}

\MetricName{} is specified and not yet measured, and every result above is selection-exposed validation on
regression tables, with one public classification table in the demonstration. The propositions describe
population limits; finite-sample retention depends on auditor recipes and synthetic size. An artifact serves
one declared target and does not replace the table for other uses. Copy, membership and attribute checks
are empirical diagnostics; they are not formal differential privacy and not HIPAA de-identification, and a
small program can still encode sensitive aggregates. Foundation-model auditors are unqualified, drift
between versions and carry license terms. A positive impact is that organizations could evaluate external
models without releasing rows; a risk is overconfidence in empirical privacy, which is why release
certification is a separate, fail-closed gate.
