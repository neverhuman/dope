\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

Throughout, $P$ is the data law, $Q=Q_A$ the program's law, $f^\star$ and $f_A$ their regression
functions, $V=\operatorname{Var}_P f^\star(X)>0$, $D_A=\lVert f_A-f^\star\rVert^2_{L^2(P_X)}$, and
$w=\mathrm{d}P_X/\mathrm{d}Q_X$ with $\lVert w\rVert_\infty<\infty$. For any $g$,
$\lVert g\rVert^2_{L^2(P_X)}=\int g^2 w\,\mathrm{d}Q_X\le\lVert w\rVert_\infty\lVert g\rVert^2_{L^2(Q_X)}$,
which is the only place the covariate law enters.

\begin{lemma}[Excess-risk decompositions]
\label{lem:decomp}
For squared loss, $L_P(h)=\mathbb{E}_P\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X)+\lVert h-f^\star\rVert^2_{L^2(P_X)}$ and the best
constant attains $\mathbb{E}_P\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X)+V$. For log-loss with conditional class probabilities
$q(\cdot\mid x)$, $L_P(q)=H_P(Y\mid X)+\mathbb{E}_{P_X}\mathrm{KL}(p^\star(\cdot\mid X)\Vert q(\cdot\mid X))$ and the
best constant, the class prior, attains $H_P(Y)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand $(Y-h)^2=(Y-f^\star)^2+2(Y-f^\star)(f^\star-h)+(f^\star-h)^2$ and take expectations; the cross
term vanishes by the tower property. A constant $c$ gives $\mathbb{E}\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X)+\lVert f^\star-c\rVert^2$,
smallest at $c=\mathbb{E}Y$, where the second term is $V$. For log-loss,
$-\log q=-\log p^\star+\log(p^\star/q)$; take conditional expectations. A constant distribution $\pi$ gives
the cross-entropy $H(Y)+\mathrm{KL}(p_Y\Vert\pi)$, smallest at the prior.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:limit}]
\emph{Squared loss.} By \cref{lem:decomp}, $L_{\mathrm{null}}-L_P(h)=V-\lVert h-f^\star\rVert^2_{L^2(P_X)}$ for
every $h$. Consistency on $P$ gives $\lVert\hat h_{P,n}-f^\star\rVert_{L^2(P_X)}\to 0$, so the denominator of
retention tends to $V$. Consistency on $Q$ gives $\lVert\hat h_{Q,N}-f_A\rVert_{L^2(Q_X)}\to0$, hence
$\lVert\hat h_{Q,N}-f_A\rVert_{L^2(P_X)}\to0$ by the density bound, and by the triangle inequality
$\lVert\hat h_{Q,N}-f^\star\rVert_{L^2(P_X)}\to\sqrt{D_A}$. The numerator tends to $V-D_A$.

\emph{Log-loss.} Assume in addition that predicted and program probabilities lie in $[c,1]$ for some
$c>0$ (DOPE's tempered softmax satisfies this on the bounded score range). By \cref{lem:decomp} the
denominator tends to $H(Y)-H(Y\mid X)=I_P(X;Y)$. For the numerator,
$\bigl|\mathbb{E}_P[-\log\hat q]-\mathbb{E}_P[-\log q_A]\bigr|\le c^{-1}\,\mathbb{E}_{P_X}\lVert\hat q(\cdot\mid X)-q_A(\cdot\mid X)\rVert_1
\le c^{-1}\lVert w\rVert_\infty\,\mathbb{E}_{Q_X}\lVert\hat q-q_A\rVert_1\to0$, since $\log$ is $c^{-1}$-Lipschitz on
$[c,1]$ and the learner is consistent on $Q$. Hence $L_{\mathrm{syn}}\to H(Y\mid X)+\mathbb{E}_{P_X}\mathrm{KL}(p^\star\Vert q_A)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:finite}]
Write $Z=\lVert\hat h_{Q,N}-f_A\rVert_{L^2(Q_X)}$, a random variable over the synthetic sample and training
randomness, with $\mathbb{E}Z^2=e_Q(N)$. By the triangle inequality and the density bound,
$\lVert\hat h_{Q,N}-f^\star\rVert_{L^2(P_X)}\le\sqrt{D_A}+\sqrt{\lVert w\rVert_\infty}\,Z$. Squaring, taking
expectations and using $\mathbb{E}Z\le\sqrt{\mathbb{E}Z^2}$,
$\mathbb{E}\lVert\hat h_{Q,N}-f^\star\rVert^2\le\bigl(\sqrt{D_A}+\sqrt{\lVert w\rVert_\infty e_Q(N)}\bigr)^2$. With
retention defined on expected losses and the null at its population value, \cref{lem:decomp} gives the
numerator $V-\mathbb{E}\lVert\hat h_{Q,N}-f^\star\rVert^2$ and the denominator $V-e_P(n)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Misspecified auditors]
\label{prop:misspec}
Let the auditor minimize empirical squared loss over a closed convex class $\mathcal{H}$, and let
$\Pi_\mu$ denote $L^2(\mu)$ projection onto $\mathcal{H}$. In the large-sample limit,
\[
R\;\to\;\frac{V-\lVert\Pi_{Q_X}f_A-f^\star\rVert^2_{L^2(P_X)}}{V-\lVert\Pi_{P_X}f^\star-f^\star\rVert^2_{L^2(P_X)}} .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The empirical risk minimizer over $\mathcal{H}$ on $Q$ converges to the minimizer of
$\mathbb{E}_Q(Y-h)^2=\mathrm{const}+\lVert h-f_A\rVert^2_{L^2(Q_X)}$, which is $\Pi_{Q_X}f_A$; likewise on $P$. Apply
\cref{lem:decomp}.
\end{proof}

For a linear class, $\Pi_{Q_X}f_A$ is determined by the second moments of $Q_X$ and the cross moments of
$X$ and $f_A(X)$ under $Q$, so it changes at first order with the covariate correlations even when
$f_A=f^\star$. This is why the dependence model matters for linear and tree auditors although it does not
enter (\ref{M-eq:limit}) for consistent learners.

\begin{lemma}[Exact marginals under the regime mixture]
\label{lem:marginals}
Let $u=M\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ and $\operatorname{diag}(MM^\top)=\mathbf{1}$, let
$k\sim\mathrm{Cat}(\pi)$ independently, and $z=\mu_k+\sigma_k\odot u$ with $\sigma_k>0$. Then
$F_j(t)=\sum_k\pi_k\Phi((t-\mu_{kj})/\sigma_{kj})$ is the distribution function of $z_j$, $F_j(z_j)$ is
uniform on $(0,1)$, and $x_j=Q_j(F_j(z_j))$ has quantile function $Q_j$, for every choice of
$(\pi,\mu,\sigma,M)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Given $k$, $u_j\sim\mathcal{N}(0,(MM^\top)_{jj})=\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, so $z_j\mid k\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{kj},\sigma_{kj}^2)$ and
$z_j$ has distribution function $F_j$, which is continuous and strictly increasing. The probability
integral transform gives uniformity, and $Q_j$ of a uniform variable has quantile function $Q_j$. For a
discrete column, $\Pr(F_j(z_j)\le F_{j,\ell})=F_{j,\ell}$ reproduces the stored cumulative probabilities.
\end{proof}

In the engine, $\Phi^{-1}\circ F_j$ is tabulated at \CfgScoreTable{} points and interpolated linearly, so
marginals are exact up to that interpolation; every dependence family stores a correlation with unit
diagonal, so the hypothesis on $M$ holds by construction.

\begin{remark}[A sampling-free retention estimate]
Replacing $f^\star$ in (\ref{M-eq:limit}) by a strong real-trained predictor $\hat g$ evaluated on held-out
rows gives $\hat R=1-\frac{\sum_i(f_A(x_i)-\hat g(x_i))^2}{\sum_i(\hat g(x_i)-\bar g)^2}$ under squared loss and
$\hat R=1-\frac{\sum_i\mathrm{KL}(\hat g(\cdot\mid x_i)\Vert q_A(\cdot\mid x_i))}{\sum_i\mathrm{KL}(\hat g(\cdot\mid x_i)\Vert\bar g)}$ under
log-loss, where $\bar g$ averages the predictions. Both are computed from the program's own conditional model
(the engine CLI's \texttt{predict} command) without sampling or retraining, and estimate the consistent-learner limit; they
are a fast screen, not a substitute for measured retention.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Greedy bit allocation]
If the added loss were separable across tensors and convex in each tensor's bit depth, and bytes linear
in bits, the greedy step of Algorithm~\ref{M-alg:bits} (most bytes saved per unit of added loss) would visit only
points on the lower convex hull of the achievable rate--distortion set, by the classical marginal-analysis
argument. The readout re-solve couples tensors, so the engine measures every candidate step rather than
predicting it.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Determinism and devices.} Random inputs are a counter-based function of the seed, a stream
index and the global row index (splitmix64 mixing, inversion by AS241 \citep{wichura1988as241}), so any
row can be generated independently and in any order. The CPU sampler evaluates rows in parallel with no
shared state. The CUDA and Metal builds evaluate the same draw as candle tensors and train with candle,
but they are not tested here; every result in the paper is from the CPU path.

\paragraph{Throughput.} \Cref{tab:throughput} reports the in-memory CPU sampler (the engine CLI's \texttt{throughput} command,
fastest of three runs of one million rows, no output written) on an otherwise idle machine
(\G{OneThroughputDevice}): from \G{OneThroughputMin} to \G{OneThroughputMax} rows per second.

\paragraph{Cost.} Compilation is dominated by training at most a few small networks, boosting one
forest per network (histograms over byte codes, rows subsampled for split search), the
rate--distortion search, and one self-audit per reserve share; each is linear in the number of rows.
A draw costs $O(dm+Kd+sh+TD)$ per row; at the default budget its working set is a few kilobytes.

\paragraph{Auditors.} Ridge and logistic regression with standardized one-hot inputs; CatBoost with
\CfgCatIters{} trees of depth \CfgCatDepth{} and default learning rate; an MLP with two hidden layers,
Adam with early stopping on larger tables and L-BFGS with stronger weight decay on small tables (the row threshold is set in
\texttt{bench/real-world/auditors.py}). Each auditor fits its own encoders on its own training rows, so a method cannot
benefit from seeing the real table's encodings.

\paragraph{Comparators.} SDV's Gaussian copula, CTGAN and TVAE use the library's detected metadata and
default hyperparameters; ARF uses arfpy defaults. Their bytes are the xz-compressed pickle of the fitted
object, which is smaller than what a deployment would ship. The research kernel runs
\texttt{dope-kernel fit-table} and \texttt{sample-table} at their defaults with the cell's seed; its bytes
are its artifact file. Every fit-and-sample call, DOPE's included,
runs under a wall-clock limit of \CfgTimeoutMin{} minutes; a call that exceeds it is recorded as a failed
cell, as are the remaining seeds of that method on that table. The compressed-rows control samples rows
without replacement and binary-searches the largest subset whose CSV fits the budget after
\texttt{xz -9e}; auditors train on those real rows.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Across tables we report medians with percentile bootstrap intervals
(\CfgDraws{} draws, tables resampled) and paired differences as Hodges--Lehmann estimates with
\CfgPairedDraws{} bootstrap draws. A table counts for a family when the real data improve on the null by at
least one percent. A fit that fails or exceeds the limit counts as robust retention zero, since a learner
given no rows can only predict the null, and stays in every denominator; per-family medians and byte
medians are taken over successful fits.

\paragraph{Compute.} Every number was produced on a two-core CPU virtual machine without accelerators.
Large neural generators and foundation-model auditors are deferred to a GPU run (Section~\ref{M-sec:limits} of the main text).
